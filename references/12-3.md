## Overview

Covers: 14. Using Grooves; 15. Using Tuning Systems.

## 14. Using Grooves

### 14. Using Grooves

You can reshape the rhythmic placement and “feel“ of any clip in your Set by applying _grooves_. A broad collection of these ships with Live, shown as .agr files inside the browser.

Groove Files in the Browser.

Working with the library grooves is simplest when you drag one straight from the browser onto a clip in your Set; the groove file's timing traits are then handed to that clip right away. To audition several grooves rapidly, switch on the Hot-Swap button sitting over a clip’s [Clip Groove chooser](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/clip-view/#clip-groove), then move through the browser's grooves as the clip keeps playing.

The Hot-Swap Groove Button.

Both MIDI and audio clips accept grooves. With audio, a groove operates by shifting the clip’s [warping behavior](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/audio-clips-tempo-and-warping/#warping), so it has an effect only where Warp is turned on.

### 14.1 Groove Pool

After a groove file has been applied, tweak how it behaves by editing its parameters within the _Groove Pool_. You open or close that pool from a drop-down entry in the browser view control, or with the CtrlAlt6 (Win) / CmdOption6 (Mac) shortcut.

The Groove Pool View Control.

Double-clicking a groove in the browser is another route: it sends the groove straight into the Groove Pool ahead of assigning it to any clip. Every groove brought in that way, plus those currently in use by clips, sits in the Groove Pool. Any “Inactive“ groove — one no clip is using — shows up with its parameters dimmed.

The Groove Pool.

### 14.1.1 Adjusting Groove Parameters

Listed within the Groove Pool, each groove exposes several parameters you can change live to reshape how the clips relying on it behave. Buttons placed beside the Groove’s name also let you save and hot-swap it.

Here is what each control in the Groove Pool does:

- _Base_ — This chooser fixes the timing grid the groove's notes are gauged against. Pick a 1/4 Base and the groove file's note positions are judged relative to the closest quarter note, with every note in the clips tied to that groove sliding proportionally toward the groove note positions. Set a 1/8th base and the groove’s notes are gauged from their closest eighth note. Where a groove note lands precisely on the grid it stays put, so the matching notes in your clips also remain in place.
- _Quantize_ — governs how much “straight“ quantization is imposed ahead of the groove. Push it to 100% and the notes in your clips snap to the closest values per the Base chooser. Leave it at 0% and clip notes keep their starting positions until the groove acts on them.
- _Timing_ — controls the degree to which the groove pattern influences whichever clips employ it.
- _Random_ — sets how much random timing jitter reaches clips that use the chosen groove. Used sparingly, it lends a faint “humanization“ to tightly quantized electronic loops. Bear in mind that Random hands a distinct randomization to each voice in your clip, so notes that once landed together end up randomly displaced from the grid and from one another.
- _Velocity_ — dictates how strongly the velocity data held in the groove file reshapes the velocity of the clip's notes. This slider spans -100 to +100. Below zero the groove’s velocity effect flips, with loud notes sounding soft and the reverse.
- _Global Amount_ — this control scales the combined strength of Timing, Random and Velocity across all of the groove files on hand. Held at 100%, the parameters take their set values. The Amount slider reaches 130%, opening the door to even more pronounced groove effects. Whenever grooves are active on clips in your Set, the Global Amount slider turns up in Live’s Control Bar too.
  The Global Groove Amount Slider in the Control Bar

### 14.1.2 Committing Grooves

The Commit Groove Button.

Hitting the Commit button above the Clip Groove chooser “writes“ the current groove parameters into the clip. On a MIDI clip the notes shift to match. On an audio clip it lays down Warp Markers at the right spots in the clip.

Once you press Commit, the clip’s Groove chooser is reset to None on its own.

### 14.2 Editing Grooves

How a groove file shapes your clips rests on two things together: the parameter values dialed in within the Groove Pool and where the notes actually sit inside the groove files. If you want to alter a groove file's contents firsthand, drag it out of the browser or Groove Pool and drop it onto a MIDI track. A fresh MIDI clip results, and you edit it just like any other MIDI clip. The reworked clip can then be turned back into a groove using the method that follows.

### 14.2.1 Extracting Grooves

A brand-new groove can be built by pulling the timing and volume data out of any MIDI or audio clip. Achieve this either by dropping the clip onto the Groove Pool or through the Extract Groove command found in the clip’s context menu.

Extract Grooves From Audio or MIDI Clips.

When a groove is built through extraction, only the audible playing region of the clip is taken into account.

### 14.3 Groove Tips

A handful of pointers for squeezing the most value from grooves follow here.

### 14.3.1 Grooving a Single Voice

To land a believable beat, drummers frequently nudge the timing of individual instruments. Keeping hi-hats on the grid while letting snare hits sit a touch late, for instance, is an effective route to a relaxed, laid-back feel. Yet since a groove file acts on a whole clip in one go, that level of nuance is hard to reach inside a single clip. If your clip runs through a Drum or Instrument Rack, one workaround is to [extract the chain](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/instrument-drum-and-effect-racks/#extracting-chains) holding the voice you wish to groove on its own. Here we'd pull out the snare chain, yielding a new clip and track carrying only the snare notes. A separate groove could then be assigned to that new clip.

### 14.3.2 Non-Destructive Quantization

You can lean on grooves to bring live, non-destructive quantization to clips. Simply drop the groove’s Timing, Random and Velocity amounts to 0% and dial its Quantize and Base parameters however you like. Since only Quantize is in play, the groove's own content plays no role, which means the approach behaves identically no matter which Groove file is chosen.

### 14.3.3 Creating Texture With Randomization

A groove’s Random parameter is handy for producing convincing doublings, which pays off especially when you're building string textures out of lone voices. Begin by duplicating the track that holds the clip you want to “thicken.“ Next, assign a groove to one of the two clips and raise its Random parameter. Play both clips at once and every note drifts a little — and randomly — away from its twin on the other track.

## 15. Using Tuning Systems

### 15. Using Tuning Systems

Out of the box Live runs on 12TET tuning, where each octave is split evenly into twelve note pitches. That said, pitches may be laid out across an octave — or a pseudo-octave, where notes recur at an interval other than an octave — in many different ways, and tuning systems exist to define such variations.

[Scala files](https://www.huygens-fokker.org/scala/) are handled by Live 12; load one into a Live Set and your notes follow a custom tuning.

A collection of tuning systems ships in the Core Library as well, located under the browser's Tunings label. Those Core Library tuning files rely on [ASCL](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/10998372840220), an Ableton-specific add-on to the SCL (Scala) file format.

The Tunings Label in the Browser.

Hover across or pick a tuning system in the browser and the Info View presents a brief blurb about that tuning, the count of notes per octave included. That same blurb appears when you hover over the name of a [loaded tuning system](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-tuning-systems/#loading-a-tuning-system).

A Tuning System Description in the Info View.

Drop your own .scl or .ascl files into any folder within Live’s Places, and they appear beneath the User tag inside the browser's Tunings label.

User-saved Tunings in the Browser.

Every instrument built into Live works with tuning systems, and so do MPE-enabled plug-ins or external Max for Live instruments as long as their pitch bend range sits at 48 semitones.

Keep in mind that an instrument lacking MPE support, or set to some other pitch bend range, will probably sound out of tune.

### 15.1 Loading a Tuning System

Bringing a tuning system into a Set is done by double-clicking a tuning file in the browser, or by highlighting the file and hitting Enter. Either approach opens the browser's Tuning section by itself, since that section stays hidden until then.

The Tuning section in the Browser.

The browser’s view control menu offers another way to reveal the Tuning section.

The Browser View Control Menu.

You may likewise drag ASCL or SCL files stored elsewhere on your computer and drop them into the Tuning section. Provided the file is present in the [Tuning section](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-tuning-systems/#the-tuning-section), its tuning gets stored alongside the Live Set.

After a tuning system joins a Live Set, the piano roll notes stop standing for ordinary MIDI notes and instead display the matching notes of the tuning.

Updated Notes in the MIDI Note Editor.

Hovering over a piano roll note surfaces helpful details in the Status Bar, including that note’s pitch and frequency. The stated pitch sounds whenever a note is triggered from the piano roll or through a keyboard.

Hover Over a Note in the Piano Roll for Pitch Information.

Normally, when you drop a tuning system or swap it for another, the existing notes hold their spots in the piano roll while the note ruler's pitches refresh to fit the new tuning. As a result, those original notes might no longer sound the same pitch.

Switch on the “Retune Set On Loading Tuning Systems” item in the Options menu, and loading or swapping a tuning system makes the notes hew tightly to the pitches of the originals, so a melody stays as faithful to the source as it can be. A dialog pops up to confirm the operation as you load a tuning system.

Existing MIDI Clips May Be Modified when Loading a Tuning System.

With automatic note retuning turned on, taking out a tuning system or moving between them can alter or wipe out original notes. The situation arises when a pair of notes that overlap in time and started on different pitches both end up assigned to a single pitch in the new tuning system, that pitch being the nearest match to each of them. Should that occur, one of the two notes might be trimmed or removed.

Bear in mind that once a tuning system is loaded the Scale Mode choosers in Clip View and the Control Bar disappear, while every [scale aware](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#scale-awareness) device has its Use Current Scale toggle switched off.

### 15.2 The Tuning Section

A range of pitch settings belonging to a tuning system can be reached through the Tuning section.

The Tuning Section Expanded.

Click the triangle beside the tuning file's name to open the section up and get at extra settings governing the lowest and highest notes for the reference pitch.

- _Tuning_ shows the tuning system's name.
- _Octave_ picks the octave of the note serving as the reference pitch.
- _Note_ picks the note within that octave serving as the reference pitch.
- _Ref. Pitch/Freq_ fixes the reference pitch's frequency. Shifting this frequency raises or lowers the pitch of every note in the Set.
- _Lowest Note_ defines what the lowest note sounds by tying it to an octave and pitch class. Adjusting the lowest note moves the highest note too, keeping the same count of notes between them.
- _Highest Note_ defines what the highest note sounds; altering the highest note likewise revises the lowest note.

Bear in mind that only the Ref. Pitch/Freq value alters the reference pitch in a way you can hear. Editing the Octave or Note values does refresh the frequency shown in the Ref. Pitch/Freq slider to fit the newly chosen notes, yet nothing audible shifts until you change the reference pitch frequency value itself. The point is to head off any abrupt pitch jumps while you set the Octave or Note values.

Use the floppy disk button just right of the reference pitch frequency to store the tuning that's loaded as an .ascl file under the browser's Tunings label.

Click the arrow button beside the Save Tuning System button to follow a link to [Ableton’s Tuning website](https://tuning.ableton.com/), which holds further details on the loaded tuning system plus an interactive editor for experimenting with variants of its pitches. Any custom tuning systems you build there can be exported as well. Keep in mind that not every tuning system has an external page, and the arrow button is greyed out when no matching link exists.

Highlight a file inside the Tuning section and hit the Delete key to clear it and revert to 12TET tuning.

### 15.3 MIDI Track Options for Tuning Systems

Loading a tuning system brings a handful of tuning-specific options into the I/O section of MIDI tracks, giving you ways to tailor your track and controller arrangements.

### 15.3.1 Bypass Tuning

Flip the Bypass Tuning toggle to make a particular MIDI track disregard a tuning system.

The Bypass Tuning Toggle.

Once it's on, the MIDI Note Editor shows 12TET tuning notes in the piano roll for whatever clips live on that track.

Bear in mind that a MIDI track holding Drum Racks skips any loaded tuning file on its own.

### 15.3.2 MIDI Controller Layouts

Because a tuning system's notes may not line up with 12TET layouts, the Track Tuning MIDI Controller Layout settings let you choose which notes a controller maps to and, when needed, build a layout of your own. That helps you re-align an external keyboard's layout with the piano roll whenever certain tunings cause the two to diverge.

The Track Tuning MIDI Controller Layout Options.

- _All Keys_ assigns the tuning system's notes across every key on the controller.
- _Black Keys Only_ assigns notes solely to the black keys. The layout centers on C#3.
- _White Keys Only_ assigns notes solely to the white keys. The layout centers on C3.
- _Closest in Pitch to Keyboard_ assigns notes to whichever key pitch sits nearest.
- _Custom Controller Layout_ hands you the means to set your own controller layout.

With Custom Controller Layout chosen in the chooser, click the … button on the right to reach the Configure MIDI Layout dialog and tune the layout settings.

The Configure MIDI Layout Dialog.

Any custom controller layouts are stored and brought back together with the Live Set.

### 15.4 Learn More About Tuning Systems

Head over to [Ableton’s Tuning website](https://tuning.ableton.com/) for deeper reading on Live’s built-in tuning systems, and to build and export your own custom tunings via interactive widgets.

The ASCL format also lets you craft and bring your own tuning systems into Live, provided you follow the [designated specifications](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/10998372840220).
