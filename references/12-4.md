## 20. Bounce to Audio

### 20. Bounce to Audio

Through bouncing, you render tracks, clips, or time selections down to audio placed on fresh audio tracks. This serves creative experimentation well: with the material now rendered, you're free to reshape it differently or try out sonic alternatives. The same feature lets you “lock in” a part, whether to keep momentum on the creative side or to ready the Set for mixing by wrapping up sound design and building a tidy device chain reserved for mix duties. A further benefit is that bouncing helps with [reducing the CPU load](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/computer-audio-resources-and-strategies/#computer-audio-resources-and-strategies), which liberates headroom for the rest of your project.

Bouncing is available from either the [Arrangement or Session View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#arrangement-and-session). Because both views draw on one shared collection of [tracks](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#tracks), whenever a bounce produces a track in one view, that same track appears in the other one too.

### 20.1 Bouncing Individual Tracks

Two routes exist for rendering to audio, supplied by the _Bounce Track in Place_ and _Bounce to New Track_ commands.

Bounce Track in Place renders a source track into a new audio track that takes the source's place. Keep in mind this command now stands in for what earlier Live versions called the Freeze and Flatten Track command (along with the Flatten command for frozen tracks). The Bounce Track in Place command is reachable from the track’s title bar or the clip context menu.

A Track Shown Before and After the Bounce Track in Place Command Has Been Applied.

Bounce to New Track renders single clips or a time selection into new audio clips on a separate track while muting the source clips or selection. Reach the Bounce to New Track command through the clip or selection context menu, or trigger it with the CtrlB (Win) / CmdB (Mac) shortcut.

The Source Track Alongside a New Track Produced by the Bounce to New Track Command.

Rendering to audio happens post-FX yet pre-mixer. So the resulting audio carries every bit of device processing while dropping the devices themselves from the track, and it leaves out mixer changes (namely volume, panning, and sends), which instead carry over into the new track’s settings.

If the time selection you bounce stretches across several Arrangement tracks, the Bounce to New Track command produces one audio track per track that has a clip selected on it.

What Bounce to New Track Produces from a Multi-Track Time Selection.

Be aware that even though the bouncing commands act on the Arrangement and Session at once, the outcome varies a bit by command. Run Bounce Track in Place from one view, say the Arrangement, and the new audio track also takes over the source track in the Session, with the clips on both the Arrangement and Session being rendered to audio. By contrast, running the Bounce to New Track command in a view, say the Session, still creates a new audio track in the Arrangement, but only the chosen Session clips get rendered to audio and the fresh Arrangement View track stays empty.

Any track or clip the bouncing commands produce adopts the name of its source track or clip with “(Bounce)” appended. When a source clip carries no name, its counterpart on the new track simply becomes “(Bounce).” The new track likewise picks up the color of the source track.

The audio files from a bounce live inside the Current Project folder at /Samples/Processed/Bounce.

### 20.2 Bouncing Group Tracks

Beyond individual tracks, Live also lets you bounce [Group Tracks](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/mixing/#group-tracks). Two commands handle Group Tracks: _Bounce Group in Place_ and _Bounce Group to New Track_.

Bounce Group in Place renders a new audio track that stands in for the entire Group Track. Three menus expose the command: a Group Track’s title bar context menu; in Session View, a group slot’s context menu; and within Arrangement View, the Group Track’s main lane context menu.

A Track Shown Before and After the Bounce Group in Place Command Has Been Applied.

Bounce Group to New Track renders single clips or a time selection taken from inside the Group Track into a new audio clip on a separate track, muting the source clips or selection. Two spots hold the Bounce Group to New Track command: in Session View, a group slot’s context menu; in Arrangement View, the Group Track’s main lane context menu. The CtrlB (Win) / CmdB (Mac) shortcut applies the command as well.

The Source Track Alongside a New Track Produced by the Bounce Group to New Track Command.

Keep in mind that bouncing Group Tracks behaves unlike bouncing individual tracks. Where the individual-track case bounces pre-mixer and transfers the source track’s mixer settings onto the bounced track, Group Tracks instead get bounced post-mixer. Practically, the audio is taken from the Main track’s output, capturing every effect and send applied within the Group while leaving out any processing on the Main track itself. Your actual results hinge on the routings: tracks routed away from the Group Track stay out of the bounce and can leave silence in the rendered audio, whereas return tracks sitting in the Group Track’s signal path make it into the bounce only when their final output lands on the Main track.

Bounce Group to New Track Results When One Track Is Routed Outside of the Group Track.

### 20.3 Pasting Bounced Audio

The _Paste Bounced Audio_ command offers another option besides bouncing onto a new track. With this command, the source material is rendered as it stands at the moment Paste Bounced Audio runs, so you can tweak that source and rapidly bounce and paste the audio again without reselecting and recopying the source each time. It comes in handy for whipping up quick variations of one piece of material through different effects, or for collapsing several selections drawn from different tracks into one track.

Bounced Audio Drawn from Several Tracks and Pasted Into a Single Track.

For pasting bounced audio, copy clips within a single Session View track, a time selection or clips in the Arrangement, or a time selection along a Group Track’s main lane in the Arrangement. The Copy command or the CtrlC (Win) / CmdC (Mac) shortcut handles this. After that, on an audio track, an empty MIDI track, or a take lane, invoke the Paste Bounced Audio command. Two ways reach it: the track’s main lane context menu, or else the CtrlAltV (Win) / CmdOptV (Mac) shortcut. Your copied content then gets bounced and pasted onto the track. Should you paste bounced audio onto a MIDI track, that track converts to an audio track automatically.

Bear in mind that deleting the copied source material grays out the Paste Bounced Audio command until you copy something new.

## 21. Comping

### 21. Comping

With comping, you can cherry-pick the strongest moments from each recorded take and merge them into one composite track (commonly called a “comp”). As you record, Live can spawn take lanes within a track, giving you a place to assemble your favorite sections. You might also keep alternate versions of a clip arrangement across several take lanes, or pull samples from your library onto take lanes and treat comping as a creative way to chop samples.

### 21.1 Take Lanes

Each audio or MIDI track in the [Arrangement View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#arrangement-view) may hold several parallel lanes. A track’s very first lane is the main lane, audible by default. On top of that, a track can carry as many take lanes as you like, though those sound only while [Audition Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/comping/#auditioning-take-lanes) is turned on.

A Track’s Main Lane.A Track’s Take Lanes.

Live generates take lanes for you when you're [recording new clips](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/comping/#recording-takes) in the Arrangement, and you can additionally [insert them manually](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/comping/#inserting-and-managing-take-lanes).

To switch take lanes between shown and hidden, you have two choices: from a track header’s context menu, pick the Show Take Lanes command; otherwise, flip the Show/Hide Take Lanes toggle in a track’s main lane.

The Show/Hide Take Lanes Toggle.

Showing or hiding take lanes is bound to CtrlAltU (Win) / CmdOptionU (Mac).

Pressing the left arrow key moves you from a take lane back to the main lane and, in doing so, folds all the take lanes.

Bear in mind that take lanes stay hidden while [Automation Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/automation-and-editing-envelopes/#drawing-and-editing-automation) is active. The Show Take Lanes command and take-lane insertion remain available during Automation Mode, but performing either one leaves the mode and reveals all take lanes.

### 21.2 Inserting and Managing Take Lanes

To add a take lane by hand to one or several selected tracks, choose the Insert Take Lane command from the Create menu. That same command is also available from a track or take lane header’s context menu, or through the shortcut ShiftAltT (Win) / ShiftOptionT (Mac). Adding a take lane will additionally unfold any current lanes that aren't yet on display.

It's worth noting that you can likewise build take lanes and drop clips into existing ones through [the Live API with Max for Live](https://docs.cycling74.com/apiref/lom/takelane/).

Duplicating take lanes is done with the CtrlD (Win) / CmdD (Mac) shortcut, or through the Duplicate command in a take lane header’s context menu.

Removing take lanes happens via the Backspace or Delete key, or through the Delete command in the Edit menu.

To clear every take lane off a track, open a track or take lane header’s context menu and reach for the Delete All Take Lanes command. Meanwhile, the Delete All Unused Take Lanes command clears out any take lanes lacking [recorded material within a comp](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/comping/#creating-a-comp) on the track’s main lane.

For vertical resizing of take lanes, two key combos grow or shrink them: Alt+ (Win) / Option+ (Mac) and Alt- (Win) / Option- (Mac). A third option holds Alt (Win) / Option (Mac) while you scroll the mouse wheel. Each of these acts on all the selected take lanes at once.

Hovering over a take lane’s edge surfaces resize handles, which likewise let you set the height of one or several selected lanes.

Rearranging take lanes inside their track works by dragging and dropping, or by holding the Ctrl (Win) / Cmd (Mac) modifier and tapping the up or down arrow key.

For renaming take lanes, the Rename command does the job, found either in the Edit menu or in a take lane header’s context menu. Its keyboard shortcut is CtrlR (Win) / CmdR (Mac). You can rename several take lanes at once, and the Tab and ShiftTab shortcuts step you between lanes and tracks as you rename.

### 21.3 Recording Takes

As you're [recording new clips](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/recording-new-clips/#recording-into-the-arrangement) in the Arrangement View, Live appends take lanes to [armed audio and MIDI tracks](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/recording-new-clips/#arming-record-enabling-tracks) on its own and fills those take lanes with clips.

When you record over clips that are already there, whether through separate passes or by recording inside a [loop](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#the-arrangement-loop), each pass gains a fresh take lane. Live reuses an existing take lane on its own whenever nothing else sits past the punch-in point.

A track’s most recent recorded clip is always duplicated onto that track’s main lane, ensuring it sounds right away during Set playback.

Keep in mind that recorded clips take on their track’s color by default. If you'd rather, set Live to hand each take its own color by switching the Clip Color toggle to Random in the Theme & Colors Settings.

### 21.4 Inserting Samples

Samples and MIDI files can be dragged onto take lanes straight from the browser or from File Explorer/Finder. With several samples chosen, hold the Ctrl (Win) / Cmd (Mac) modifier key as you drag so each sample lands on consecutive tracks.

### 21.5 Auditioning Take Lanes

To audition a take lane, click the Audition Take Lane button (shown as a speaker icon) within that take lane’s header, or press the T keyboard shortcut.

The Audition Take Lane Button.

Bear in mind that although take lanes belonging to different tracks can be auditioned together, each track allows only one take lane to be auditioned. Where a time selection or a lane header selection spans several take lanes across different tracks, the lane chosen last on each track is the one auditioned.

### 21.6 Creating a Comp

Whatever you've selected on take lanes can be sent to the main lane either by hitting the Enter key or through a take lane’s Copy Selection to Main Lane context menu command.

You can swap the clips on a track’s main lane for the next or previous take lane clips: select clip headers or make a time selection inside the main lane or a take lane, then hold Ctrl (Win) / Cmd (Mac) and press the up or down arrow key. When the time selection sits on a take lane, the selection itself advances to the next or previous take as well. Bear in mind that empty take lanes get skipped.

Swapping Part of a Clip for Content Taken from Another Take Lane.

While in Draw Mode, the take lane material you've selected can land on a track’s main lane in a single motion: click, drag, then let go of the mouse. You can also overwrite the clips inside a main-lane time selection by clicking a take lane, which drops that take’s matching portion into the selection.

Whatever clips arrive on a track’s main lane are standalone copies of their take lane counterparts. As a result, you can edit main-lane clips however you wish without touching the original take lane clips, and the reverse holds too. Take lane clips accept the same editing as any other Arrangement View clip: moving, copy/paste, drag/drop, consolidating, cropping, or duplicating. You can also shift take lane clips into Session View clip slots either by copy and paste or by drag and drop.

To avoid clicks where clips meet, head to the Record, Warp & Launch Settings and switch on the Create Fades on Clip Edges option there. With it on, four-millisecond crossfades appear automatically between neighboring clips. You can also build those crossfades by hand: select several clips and press CtrlAltF (Win) / CmdOptionF (Mac).

### 21.7 Source Highlights

Wherever a comp sits in a main lane, the matching source material across its take lanes appears tinted in the track’s color. Take lane material that goes unused shows up in a muted, desaturated version of that same track color. The contrast makes it simpler to spot which recordings feed into the comp. Bear in mind that source highlights appear only when the clips line up in position and properties.

A Comp’s Source Material Highlighted Across a Take Lane.

By dragging a highlight’s edge, you can resize the source highlights on take lanes and thereby shift the split point separating two neighboring parts of a comp.

Drag to Shift the Split Point Between Two Neighboring Parts of a Comp.
