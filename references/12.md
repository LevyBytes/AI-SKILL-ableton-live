### 1. Welcome to Live

Live grew out of musicians who were after a better approach to composing, producing, and performing on a computer. ## 2. First Steps

### 2. First Steps

### 2.1 Installation and Authorization

Prior to diving into Live, confirm that the most current build is set up and authorized. Grab the Live installer from your ableton.com [User Account](https://www.ableton.com/account/), then run it to [install the software](https://help.ableton.com/hc/articles/209773565). With the install complete, proceed to [authorize Live](https://help.ableton.com/hc/articles/209773585). Keep in mind that every [Live license](https://www.ableton.com/en/eula/) grants two authorizations, letting you run the program across as many as two machines, as long as just one of them is active at any moment.

### 2.2 Learning About Live

A range of resources exists both inside Live and beyond it, all aimed at helping you grow comfortable with the program and with making music more broadly.

Two learning aids ship inside Live itself: the [Learn View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/first-steps/#learn-view), which offers guided lessons, plus the [Info View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/first-steps/#info-view), which gives context-sensitive help. If you want to dig deeper, additional [resources available outside of the application](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/first-steps/#other-learning-resources) exist too. Among them are this manual, video content, and interactive web apps.

### 2.2.1 Learn View

Inside Live’s Learn View sit organized _learning modules_ that pair video material with written walk-throughs, leading you through Live’s interface and core features one stage at a time. Fresh modules keep arriving over time and slot into the Learn View whenever they appear, meaning you needn’t hold out for a Live update before reaching new material. Down at the foot of Learn View’s home page you’ll spot links pointing to this manual along with the [Knowledge Base](https://help.ableton.com).

Learn View’s Home Page.

When you launch Live for the first time, the Learn View appears automatically, though you may dismiss it through the X button at the view’s upper left corner and bring it back from the Help menu or via the CtrlAlt7 (Win) / CmdOption7 (Mac) keyboard shortcut. Provided you haven’t quit Live in between, the Learn View reopens on whichever page you saw last, letting you resume a lesson whenever you like.

Every one of the Learn View’s learning modules holds a collection of interactive lessons. Lessons can be filtered by topic and flagged as finished so you can keep tabs on how far you’ve come.

A single lesson is made up of a video walkthrough, a short write-up covering the lesson’s central ideas, plus links to further lessons at its foot (shown only while the home page is configured to list all lessons). To leave a lesson and head back to Learn View’s home page, click the _Learn View Home Page_ button at the view’s upper right corner or hit _< Lessons_ near the lesson’s top.

The _Complete Lesson_ button found at a lesson’s foot lets you flag it as finished. On the home page, lessons you’ve finished carry a check mark symbol.

Completed Lessons in One of the Learning Modules.

To view the video held inside a lesson, rely on the controls along the base of the video player. The leftmost button plays or pauses playback, the progress bar lets you scrub, and you can also change the video’s volume, switch on subtitles, or pop the video into a picture-in-picture window. Be aware that each lesson video can likewise be reached through the [Learn View playlist](https://m.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoh4MB-kbBmItuNKzdeg8rd6iUxwRdr38) on YouTube.

Sending a video into the picture-in-picture window lets you resize it and drag it anywhere on screen, so following the lesson’s steps becomes simpler while playback continues. To send a video into the picture-in-picture window, click the button just right of the video progress bar at the base. Doing so also shuts the Learn View on its own.

Use the Toggle to Open the Picture-in-Picture Window.

Within the picture-in-picture window, scrubbing happens either through the progress bar or by clicking the _← 15_ or _15 →_ buttons. Both the progress bar and those buttons show up once your mouse hovers over the video. So long as Live stays open, returning to a lesson picks the video back up at the point you stopped, even after shutting the Learn View or picture-in-picture window mid-playback.

Shutting the picture-in-picture window means hovering over it and then clicking the X button at its upper left corner. As another route, click the _Learn View →_ button to bring the Learn View back.

Close the Picture-in-Picture Window or Reopen the Learn View.

Bear in mind the picture-in-picture window stays above every application window, even after you move from Live across to some other application. Clicking the Learn View → button inside the picture-in-picture window while it floats over another application pulls Live back into focus, reopens the Learn View, and dismisses the picture-in-picture window.

Learn View’s Picture-in-Picture Window Floating Above a Browser.

An Internet connection is required for the Learn View to function. Should your network drop and later come back, click the _Reload_ button to refresh the view.

Press the Reload Button to Refresh the Learn View.

### 2.2.2 Info View

Whatever UI element your mouse hovers over, the Info View shows its name together with what it does.

Toggling the Info View on or off happens through the view control toggle down in the bottom left corner of Live’s window, or by pressing the ? key.

The Info View and its Show/Hide Button.

Beyond that, you’re free to attach your own text notes to tracks, clips, devices, and more by choosing “Edit Info Text” from the context menu of the item in question. Whatever you then type into the Info View gets stored and shown within your Live Set.

### 2.2.3 Other Learning Resources

Although getting acquainted with every part of the manual is worthwhile, the chapter we’d single out is [Live Concepts](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#live-concepts), since it sums up the whole of what Live is and is capable of. Every chapter that follows acts as a fuller reference for the topics first raised in Live Concepts.

Should you want to hear fellow musicians describe their workflows and Live rigs, or to learn about other Ableton products, the official [Ableton YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@Ableton) is worth a visit.

Ableton additionally runs a pair of websites, [Learning Music](https://learningmusic.ableton.com) and [Learning Synths](https://learningsynths.ableton.com), addressing the fundamentals of making music and of working with synthesizers respectively. Both sites walk you through a sequence of interactive steps that introduce ideas bit by bit while letting you test out each thing right away. There’s no need for prior know-how or gear; everything plays out straight inside your browser.

### 2.3 Live’s Settings

Tailoring Live’s many preferences happens in the Settings window. On Windows it lives in the Options menu, while on macOS it sits in the Live menu. The Settings window can also be summoned with the shortcut Ctrl, (Win) / Cmd, (Mac).

Spread across a number of tabs, Live’s Settings are each covered below.

### 2.3.1 Display & Input

Within the Display & Input Settings you’ll find controls for language and zoom, plus keyboard navigation and assorted other application preferences.

- Through the Display section you pick the language you’d like for Live’s UI and dial in the zoom level for Live’s main window (plus a second window, should one be open).
- Display Options is where you can switch on “Outline View in Focus,” which draws a border around whichever view is active at the moment. It also lets you decide how scroll bars look, configure the Follow behavior for [Arrangement View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#navigation-and-zooming) and [clips](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/clip-view/#follow-action-and-launch-controls), and reveal or conceal user interface labels.
- The Navigation and Keyboard section unlocks several keyboard workflow choices, among them using the Tab key to shift keyboard focus, letting the Tab key cycle through a section without stopping, and moving clips with the arrow keys.
- Mouse and Pen Input is where you turn on Pen Tablet Mode along with permanent scrub areas.
- Any “Don’t Show Again” warning dialogs you’d formerly dismissed can be brought back from the Application Options section.

### 2.3.2 Theme & Colors

Live’s whole visual presentation is settled within the Theme & Color Settings.

- The Theme section is where you pick a color scheme, or instead let Live mirror the light/dark mode coming from your OS. You may additionally give the palette a warm, cool, or neutral cast, and switch on high contrast where it’s wanted.
- Through the Customization tab you regulate how opaque the UI’s grid lines appear, tune the brightness level, and dial in color intensity and hue.
- Within the Track and Clip Colors section, auto-assigning track colors can be toggled on or off, or you can settle on one default color for every track. Setting the Clip Color toggle either produces random colors or borrows the track’s color for fresh clips.

### 2.3.3 Audio

By way of the Audio Settings, you can establish how Live talks to the outside world through an audio interface. That covers reaching individual input/output devices, sample rate and latency controls, plus a testing section meant for audio interface calibration.

Under macOS, the Use System Device option within the audio output chooser ties Live’s output device to whatever the macOS Sound System Settings currently select. As a result, Live and your operating system end up sharing one device without extra effort.

The Use System Device Option on macOS.

### 2.3.4 Link

Setting up Ableton Link is handled through the Link Settings. For deeper detail on putting Link to work inside Live, turn to the [Synchronizing via Link](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/synchronizing-with-link-tempo-follower-and-midi/#synchronizing-via-link) section within the Synchronizing with Link, Tempo Follower, and MIDI chapter.

### 2.3.5 Tempo & MIDI

By way of the Tempo & MIDI Settings, Live is taught to identify external devices across three separate and distinct purposes:

- Locking the program to external devices through Tempo Follower or MIDI. Consult the [Synchronizing via Tempo Follower](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/synchronizing-with-link-tempo-follower-and-midi/#synchronizing-via-tempo-follower) and [Synchronizing via MIDI](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/synchronizing-with-link-tempo-follower-and-midi/#synchronizing-via-midi) sections if you’d like fuller detail.
- Sounding MIDI notes. For guidance on bringing an external device into Live as MIDI input, or on dispatching MIDI out to an external device, refer to the [Routing and I/O](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/routing-and-i-o/#external-midi-inout) chapter.
- Steering portions of the interface from afar. That topic gets thorough treatment in the [MIDI and Key Remote Control](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/midi-and-key-remote-control/#midi-and-key-remote-control) chapter.

### 2.3.6 File & Folder

Preferences tied to how data is handled, to custom [Max for Live paths](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/max-for-live/#max-for-live), and to [Live’s decoding cache](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/#managing-files-and-sets) all live within the File & Folder Settings.

### 2.3.7 Library

Through the Library Settings you set a default home for several kinds of installed files, Packs and your User Library among them, and decide whether samples and other files travel self-contained whenever you’re [saving Sets](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/#collect-files-on-export). They also let you reveal or hide the synchronization options for [Ableton Cloud](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-the-browser/#using-ableton-cloud) and [Push](https://www.ableton.com/en/push/manual/#continuity-between-push-and-live) inside Live’s Places.

### 2.3.8 Plug-Ins

Within the Plug-Ins Settings you point Live at where your plug-in folders sit, mark which of those folders it should draw on, and adjust how [plug-in windows](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-instruments-and-effects/#using-plug-ins) behave on screen.

### 2.3.9 Record, Warp & Launch

By way of the Record, Warp & Launch Settings, you tailor how fresh [Live Sets](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/#live-sets) and the pieces inside them start out, and you also pick the choices governing [new recordings](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/recording-new-clips/#recording-new-clips).

### 2.3.10 Licenses & Updates

Handling authorization, automatic updates, and usage data is what the Licenses & Updates Settings are for.
