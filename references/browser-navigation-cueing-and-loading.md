### 4.8 Navigating in the Browser

Getting around the files in Live’s browser works with either the mouse or your computer keyboard:

- Move up and down within a browser label using the up and down arrow keys, the mousewheel, or a click-and-drag while holding the Ctrl+Alt (Win) / Cmd+Option (Mac) modifier.
- Use the left and right arrow keys to collapse and expand folders or to shift between the sidebar and content pane.

By default, opening a new folder closes whatever folders were already open, but holding Ctrl (Win) / Cmd (Mac) as you open new folders overrides that.

### 4.9 Previewing Files

Samples, clips, and instrument presets can all be previewed by Live right inside the browser. That gives you a sense of how a thing sounds before you commit a new element to your Set.

Switch on previewing by turning on the Preview toggle beside the Preview Tab at the foot of the browser.

The Preview Toggle.

With it switched on, selecting a file in the browser plays a preview. The up and down arrow keys step you through files and their audio previews.

Even with the Preview toggle switched off, you can still preview files by pressing Shift+Enter or the right arrow key while a browser item is selected.

As a file previews, the Preview Tab displays a waveform. Clicking within the scrub area jumps playback to that spot. Be aware that scrubbing isn't available for clips saved with Warp switched off.

The Preview Tab’s Scrub Area.

The Raw button starts out switched off, letting Live begin previewing files at the top of the next bar while transport is running. That helps you judge how the sound sits within your current project. Switching on the Raw button previews files at their native tempo. Audio previews loop while Raw is off and play unlooped while Raw is on.

Keep in mind that scrubbing can't be done while Raw is on.

The volume of audio previews is set with the Main track’s Preview/Cue Volume knob.

The Preview/Cue Volume Knob.

Should your audio interface offer four or more dedicated outputs — or two dedicated stereo pairs — headphones plugged into a separate pair of outs let you audition or cue files, all while the track keeps sounding through the Main outputs. For guidance on configuring Live to cue, turn to the [Soloing and Cueing](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/mixing/#soloing-and-cueing) part of the Mixing chapter.

### 4.10 Adding Content from the Browser to a Live Set

Browser content can be brought into a Live Set through a number of approaches:

- You can drag items out of the browser and drop them onto tracks within either the Session or Arrangement View. When you instead drop browser content into the area to the right of Session View tracks, or beneath the Arrangement View tracks, Live builds a fresh track and deposits the new item(s) inside it.
- Within the Session View, a double-click — or a press of Enter — on a browser device drops it onto whichever track is active. The same gesture applied to a sample likewise sends it to the active track. For MIDI tracks a sample arrives wrapped in a Simpler device, whereas on audio tracks it lands in a clip slot.
- Over in the Arrangement View, double-clicking a browser device or sample (or hitting Enter on it) places that item onto whichever track happens to be selected at the time.
- It is equally possible to drag files straight into Live out of the Explorer (Win)/Finder (Mac).
