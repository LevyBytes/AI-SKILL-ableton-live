### 2.2.1 Learn View

Inside Live’s Learn View sit organized _learning modules_ that pair video material with written walk-throughs, leading you through Live’s interface and core features one stage at a time. Fresh modules keep arriving over time and slot into the Learn View whenever they appear, meaning you needn’t hold out for a Live update before reaching new material. Down at the foot of Learn View’s home page you’ll spot links pointing to this manual along with the [Knowledge Base](https://help.ableton.com).

Learn View’s Home Page.

When you launch Live for the first time, the Learn View appears automatically, though you may dismiss it through the X button at the view’s upper left corner and bring it back from the Help menu or via the Ctrl+Alt+7 (Win) / Cmd+Option+7 (Mac) keyboard shortcut. Provided you haven’t quit Live in between, the Learn View reopens on whichever page you saw last, letting you resume a lesson whenever you like.

Every one of the Learn View’s learning modules holds a collection of interactive lessons. Lessons can be filtered by topic and flagged as finished so you can keep tabs on how far you’ve come.

A single lesson is made up of a video walkthrough, a short write-up covering the lesson’s central ideas, plus links to further lessons at its foot (shown only while the home page is configured to list all lessons). To leave a lesson and head back to Learn View’s home page, click the _Learn View Home Page_ button at the view’s upper right corner or hit _< Lessons_ near the lesson’s top.

The _Complete Lesson_ button found at a lesson’s foot lets you flag it as finished. On the home page, lessons you’ve finished carry a check mark symbol.

Completed Lessons in One of the Learning Modules.

To view the video held inside a lesson, rely on the controls along the base of the video player. The leftmost button plays or pauses playback, the progress bar lets you scrub, and you can also change the video’s volume, switch on subtitles, or pop the video into a picture-in-picture window. Be aware that each lesson video can likewise be reached through the [Learn View playlist](https://m.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoh4MB-kbBmItuNKzdeg8rd6iUxwRdr38) on YouTube.

Sending a video into the picture-in-picture window lets you resize it and drag it anywhere on screen, so following the lesson’s steps becomes simpler while playback continues. To send a video into the picture-in-picture window, click the button just right of the video progress bar at the base. Doing so also shuts the Learn View on its own.

Use the Toggle to Open the Picture-in-Picture Window.

Within the picture-in-picture window, scrubbing happens either through the progress bar or by clicking the _← 15_ or _15 →_ buttons. Both the progress bar and those buttons show up once your mouse hovers over the video. So long as Live stays open, returning to a lesson picks the video back up at the point you stopped, even after shutting the Learn View or picture-in-picture window mid-playback.

Shutting the picture-in-picture window means hovering over it and then clicking the X button at its upper left corner. As another route, click the _Learn View →_ button to bring the Learn View back.

Close the Picture-in-Picture Window or Reopen the Learn View.

Bear in mind the picture-in-picture window stays above every application window, even after you move from Live across to some other application. Clicking the Learn View → button inside the picture-in-picture window while it floats over another application pulls Live back into focus, reopens the Learn View, and dismisses the picture-in-picture window.

Learn View’s Picture-in-Picture Window Floating Above a Browser.

An Internet connection is required for the Learn View to function. Should your network drop and later come back, click the _Reload_ button to refresh the view.

Press the Reload Button to Refresh the Learn View.

### 2.2.2 Info View

Whatever UI element your mouse hovers over, the Info View shows its name together with what it does.

Toggling the Info View on or off happens through the view control toggle down in the bottom left corner of Live’s window, or by pressing the ? key.

The Info View and its Show/Hide Button.

Beyond that, you’re free to attach your own text notes to tracks, clips, devices, and more by choosing “Edit Info Text” from the context menu of the item in question. Whatever you then type into the Info View gets stored and shown within your Live Set.

### 2.2.3 Other Learning Resources

Although getting acquainted with every part of the manual is worthwhile, the chapter we’d single out is [Live Concepts](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#live-concepts), since it sums up the whole of what Live is and is capable of. Every chapter that follows acts as a fuller reference for the topics first raised in Live Concepts.

Should you want to hear fellow musicians describe their workflows and Live rigs, or to learn about other Ableton products, the official [Ableton YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@Ableton) is worth a visit.

Ableton additionally runs a pair of websites, [Learning Music](https://learningmusic.ableton.com) and [Learning Synths](https://learningsynths.ableton.com), addressing the fundamentals of making music and of working with synthesizers respectively. Both sites walk you through a sequence of interactive steps that introduce ideas bit by bit while letting you test out each thing right away. There’s no need for prior know-how or gear; everything plays out straight inside your browser.
