# Live Concepts (part 1)

> Ableton Live reference. Original prose; identifiers preserved verbatim.

### 3. Live Concepts

Here we lay out the core ideas behind Live. Reading these pages near the start of your time with the program is worth your while, since grasping the fundamentals up front lets you tap the software's full range when you make music.

### 3.1 The Control Bar

Live's playback and tempo handles live on the Control Bar, alongside extra widgets that let you tweak scale and MIDI behavior, watch CPU load, and flip back and forth between the Session and Arrangement View.

The Control Bar.

Nine distinct sections make up the Control Bar:

_Browser Options_ - Here sits the Show/Hide Browser toggle, used to reveal or conceal Live's browser, plus the Browser Config Menu, whose choices let you stretch the browser to full height and surface the Tuning and Groove Pool sections.

_Tempo Settings and Metronome_ - This area gives you the means to switch on [Link](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/synchronizing-with-link-tempo-follower-and-midi/#synchronizing-via-link), dial in your Set's [tempo](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/audio-clips-tempo-and-warping/#tempo) along with the time signature, adjust [the metronome](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/recording-new-clips/#metronome-settings), and switch [Tempo Follower](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/synchronizing-with-link-tempo-follower-and-midi/#synchronizing-via-tempo-follower) on or off.

_Scale Settings_ - Whatever [scale settings](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#scale-awareness) the active clip carries are mirrored here. Whatever you adjust in this area carries over to the clip or clip slot you've highlighted, plus any clips you make afterward and any clip slots you go on to choose.

_Follow and Arrangement Position_ - From here you can switch [Follow](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#navigation-and-zooming) on or off, plus inspect and tweak where you currently sit in the Arrangement.

_Transport Controls_ - The widgets that begin or halt playback, and that kick off Arrangement recording, sit in this section.

_Automation and Capture MIDI_ - Found here are the means to adjust MIDI overdub behavior, arm [automation](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/automation-and-editing-envelopes/#automation-and-editing-envelopes), restore automation for parameters you've currently overriden, [capture MIDI](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/recording-new-clips/#capturing-midi), and begin Session recording.

_Arrangement Loop Settings_ - Switching on and setting up [the Arrangement loop](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#the-arrangement-loop), plus the recording punch-in and punch-out points, happens in this section.

_MIDI and CPU Settings_ - This area lets you switch on [Draw Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/editing-midi/#draw-mode), turn on the [Computer MIDI Keyboard](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-keyboard-shortcuts/#keymidi-map-mode-and-the-computer-midi-keyboard), flip [Key and MIDI map modes](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/midi-and-key-remote-control/#midi-and-key-remote-control) on or off, set the project sample rate, and keep an eye on CPU load.

_View Selector_ - A single toggle here flips you between the Session and Arrangement View.

### 3.2 The Status Bar

Handy notices show up on the Status Bar, such as error notices or news about new releases on offer (provided Automatic Updates is switched on in the [Licenses & Updates Settings](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/first-steps/#licenses--updates)).

The Status Bar.

While you edit in the MIDI Note Editor, the Status Bar reports useful facts about a chosen note's spot, pitch, velocity, and probability. Hover the pointer above an insert marker in the Session or Arrangement View and the Status Bar reports exactly where that marker sits.

### 3.3 The Browser

Your collection of musical material is reached through Live's [browser](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-the-browser/#working-with-the-browser): the Core Library of sounds shipped with the program, extra sounds you brought in through _Live Packs_, presets and samples you've stored, devices both built-in and third-party, Sets you keep in [Ableton Cloud](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-the-browser/#using-ableton-cloud), files held on Push, and whatever folders you've appended yourself.

The Browser.

To reveal or conceal the browser, use the dedicated Show/Hide Browser toggle on the Control Bar, or press CtrlAlt5 (Win) / CmdOption5 (Mac). The Browser Config Menu likewise offers choices for surfacing more sections or stretching the browser to full height.

### 3.4 Sound Similarity

Built into Live is sound similarity recognition, which powers a pair of features: Similarity Search and Similar Sample Swapping. Similarity Search locates sounds that resemble a reference file by matching that reference against entries in the Core Library and the User Library. Samples as long as 60 seconds, instrument presets, and drum presets all work with Similarity Search. You can pair it with Live's Similar Sample Swapping feature to substitute sample files with comparable sounds inside Drum Rack, Simpler, or Drum Sampler.

Whenever a compatible file is highlighted in the browser, a Show Similar Files icon shows up beside it.

The Show Similar Files Icon Next to a Sample.

A click on the icon produces a roster of sounds resembling the reference file. Alternatively, right-click an entry and pick Show Similar Files, or press the CtrlShiftF (Win) / CmdShift5F (Mac) shortcut, to call up that roster. Your reference file appears in the search field, with every matching, similar-sounding entry listed underneath it, sorted from closest match to most distant.

Similarity Search Results.

If you store custom User Labels drawn from a set of Similarity Search results, the sound file the search started from gets placed automatically at the head of the list.

For the similarity features to function, sound content must first be analyzed. That analysis runs quietly behind the scenes each time Live turns up fresh user audio files. While background scanning and analysis run, the Status Bar reports where Live's similarity analysis stands, and a Pause button beside the analysis state lets you halt the procedure whenever you like. Keep in mind that content in the Core Library arrives already analyzed.

### 3.5 Live Sets

What you build and edit within Live is a document termed a [Live Set](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/#live-sets). Every Live Set lives inside a Live Project — a folder that gathers the materials that belong together. After you store the Project folder, the File menu's Open command lets you reopen the Set later.

A Live Set in the Browser.

You can also reach the Live Project folder and the related files tied to whichever Live Set is open right now through the Current Project label inside Live's Places.

### 3.6 Arrangement and Session

Live's fundamental musical units go by the name _clips_. Each clip holds some musical content: a melody, a drum groove, a bassline, or even a finished track. With Live you can capture and reshape clips, then assemble bigger musical forms out of them: songs, scores, remixes, DJ sets, or live performances.

Clips can be handled in a pair of views: the _Arrangement_, where clips are arranged along a musical timeline that runs linearly; and the _Session_, a real-time-oriented "launching base" for clips. A dedicated play button sits on every Session clip, letting you fire that clip whenever and in whatever sequence you wish. How each clip acts once fired can be set exactly through a [number of settings](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/launching-clips/#launching-clips).

Clips in the Arrangement View (Left) and in the Session View (Right).

You reach the Arrangement by way of the [Arrangement View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#arrangement-view), and the Session by way of the [Session View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/session-view/#session-view).

Running Live in just one window, you flip between the pair of views with the computer's Tab key or with the matching view controls up in the window's top right corner. Running two windows instead, a press of Tab moves the Session and Arrangement from one window across to the other. Bear in mind that with the 'Use Tab Key to Navigate' option switched on in the Display & Input Settings, Tab will no longer flip between Arrangement and Session View. Even so, the shortcuts Alt1 (Win) / Option1 (Mac) take you to Session View and Alt2 (Win) / Option2 (Mac) take you to Arrangement View. Their Navigate menu entries likewise let you move between the views at any moment.

The Arrangement and Session View Controls.

Since the pair of views serve separate purposes, each keeps its own separate set of clips. Still, it's worth grasping that flipping between the views while playing or recording alters only the UI — the clips currently sounding keep going untouched.

There are handy ways the Arrangement View and Session View work together. For example, you might jam with Session clips while you [record a log of the improvisation](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/session-view/#recording-sessions-into-the-arrangement) into the Arrangement, then polish it afterward. This is possible because _tracks_ tie Arrangement and Session together.

### 3.7 Tracks

Clips live on tracks, which also govern how signals move and how fresh clips come into being through recording, sound synthesis, effects processing, and mixing.

A Track in the Arrangement View.

One common pool of tracks is shared by the Session and the Arrangement. Within the Session View, tracks line up as columns, whereas within the Arrangement View they stack one above another, with time running from the left toward the right.

Only a single clip plays on a track at any given moment. That's why clips meant to play as alternatives usually go in one Session View column, while clips meant to sound together get spread across tracks into rows, which we term [scenes](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/session-view/#tracks-and-scenes).

A Scene in the Session View.

A track at any given moment plays either a Session clip or an Arrangement clip, yet never the two together. Priority goes to Session clips. Firing a Session clip halts whatever is currently sounding so the freshly launched clip can play. Specifically, an Arrangement clip playing on that track will cease so the Session clip can sound instead — meanwhile the remaining tracks carry on playing their Arrangement clips. On the track where you fired the Session clip, the Arrangement clips stay silent until you restart them by hand with the _Back to Arrangement_ button.

You'll find the Back to Arrangement button on the _Main track_ within the Session View, plus at the upper right of the scrub area within the Arrangement View. Its lit state signals that one or more tracks are at that moment _not_ following the Arrangement, but are instead sounding a clip out of the Session.

Session View's Back to Arrangement Button.Arrangement View's Back to Arrangement Button.

Pressing this button returns every track to playing the Arrangement. Within the Arrangement View, each track carries its own Back to Arrangement button too, so you can bring just some tracks back to Arrangement playback.

A Single Track's Back to Arrangement Button.

There's also the option of committing the present Session state into the Arrangement, done by switching on the _Arrangement Record_ button from within the Session View.

The Arrangement Record Button.

By recording into the Arrangement tracks, you can cut several [takes](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/comping/#recording-takes) of a clip and afterward assemble them into a single [composite track](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/comping/#comping).

Tracks can also be [linked](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#linked-track-editing) so that one action applies across several tracks together.

Creating a Fade in Two Linked Tracks.

### 3.8 Audio and MIDI

Two kinds of signal occupy Live: audio and MIDI. Digitally speaking, an audio signal amounts to a string of numbers standing in for a smooth waveform. That signal might come from any number of places — a microphone's audio, a sound built or sampled in software, or a feed sent out to a loudspeaker. A MIDI signal, by contrast, is a string of commands, along the lines of "now play a C4 at mezzo piano." MIDI stands as a symbolic depiction of musical content, nearer to notation on paper than to a recorded sound. Hardware input gear such as MIDI or USB keyboards, or software devices, produce MIDI signals.

Turning MIDI signals into audio signals you can actually hear takes an [instrument](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-instruments-and-effects/#working-with-instruments-and-effects). Certain instruments, Live's [Simpler](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-instrument-reference/#simpler) among them, exist for chromatic performance of a single sound across the keyboard. Others, such as Live's [Impulse](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-instrument-reference/#impulse), place a separate percussion sound under each key.

Capturing and playing audio signals is the job of _audio tracks_, while capturing and playing MIDI signals is the job of _MIDI tracks_. A matching clip type belongs to each of the two track types. You cannot put audio clips onto MIDI tracks, nor the reverse.

Details on adding, rearranging, and removing audio and MIDI tracks appear in the [Audio and MIDI Tracks](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/mixing/#audio-and-midi-tracks) section of the Mixing chapter.

### 3.9 Audio Clips and Samples

Inside an audio clip is a pointer to a _sample_ (which you might also call a "sound file" or "audio file") or to a _compressed sample_ (an MP3 file, say). That clip records where on your drives the sample sits, which portion of the sample should play, and _how_ it ought to play.

Drag a sample in from Live's built-in browser and Live builds a clip on its own to sound that sample. Before you drag a sample in, you can audition or _preview_ it right inside the browser. With the Browser File Preview button bearing the headphones icon switched on, the preview begins on its own the instant you pick a sample.

A Selected Sample with Audio Preview in the Browser.

Live gives you a wealth of ways to play samples in fresh and surprising fashions, so you can conjure a wide array of new sounds while the original sample stays untouched — every alteration is figured in real time as the sample sounds. You'll find those settings in the [_Clip View_](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#clip-and-device-view), which appears once you double-click a clip.

An Audio Clip's Properties as Displayed in the Clip View.

A great deal of this power flows from Live's [warping capabilities](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/audio-clips-tempo-and-warping/#warping). To warp is to alter how fast a sample plays apart from its pitch, in order to fit the song tempo. You can shift the tempo on the fly using the Control Bar's Tempo field.

The Control Bar's Tempo Field.

The simplest application of this approach, which generally needs no hand-tuning at all, is locking sample loops to whatever tempo you've picked. Indeed, Live's Auto-Warp algorithm makes it straightforward to align _any_ sample with the song tempo, even a recording of some drunken jazz band's playing. Pushing the warp settings to extremes can also reshape a sound's sonic character drastically.

### 3.10 MIDI Clips and MIDI Files

Within a MIDI clip lives musical content expressed as MIDI notes and controller envelopes. Bring MIDI in from a [MIDI file](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/#midi-files) and that data folds into the Live Set, with the source file no longer referenced from then on. Inside Live's browser, a MIDI file shows up with a distinct icon and the .mid file extension.

A MIDI File in the Browser.

What a MIDI clip holds can be [accessed and edited](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/editing-midi/#editing-midi) through the Clip View — to reshape a melody or build a drum groove, for example.

A MIDI Clip's Properties as Displayed in the Clip View.

Beyond capturing MIDI signals arriving from outside gear, Live also lets you place MIDI notes into clips with [Draw Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/editing-midi/#draw-mode), [MIDI Tools](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/midi-tools/#midi-tools), or [audio-to-MIDI converters](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/converting-audio-to-midi/#converting-audio-to-midi).

### 3.11 Devices

Beyond clips, a track may also hold a [chain of devices](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-instruments-and-effects/#working-with-instruments-and-effects) that process signals. A double-click on a track's title bar opens the [_Device View_](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#clip-and-device-view), where that track's device chain is shown.

The Device View Displaying a MIDI Track's Device Chain.

The devices that take in and put out audio signals carry the name [_audio effects_](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-audio-effect-reference/#live-audio-effect-reference). Among device types, only audio effects belong on an audio track or a return track. MIDI tracks, though, accept two further device types: [_MIDI effects_](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-midi-effect-reference/#live-midi-effect-reference) and [_instruments_](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-instrument-reference/#live-instrument-reference).

The browser is where you'll find Live's built-in audio effects, MIDI effects, and instruments. Tracks gain devices when you drag them out of the browser into the Device View, or drop them onto a Session or Arrangement track. You can also pick instruments and effects in the browser and press Enter to load them onto a track.

Live's Built-in Devices Are Available from the Browser.

[Plug-in devices](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-instruments-and-effects/#using-plug-ins) are usable in Live as well. The browser's _Plug-Ins_ label is where VST and Audio Units (macOS only) plug-ins reside.

Plug-In Devices Are Available from the Browser's Plug-Ins Label.

### 3.12 Clip and Device View

Setting and tuning clip properties — start or end points, looping, scale settings — is what the [Clip View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/clip-view/#clip-view) is for. From the Session View, it additionally opens up extended clip properties such as [follow actions](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/launching-clips/#follow-actions).

The Clip View

With audio clips, the Clip View opens up [warping controls](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/audio-clips-tempo-and-warping/#warping) plus [audio transformation tools](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/audio-clips-tempo-and-warping/#quantizing-audio).

With MIDI clips, the Clip View brings pitch and time utilities, together with MIDI [Transformation](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/midi-tools/#transformation-tools) and [Generative tools](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/midi-tools/#generative-tools).

A roster of the devices loaded on whichever track you've selected appears in the [Device View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-instruments-and-effects/#device-view). On MIDI tracks you can load MIDI effects, instruments, and audio effects. On audio, group, and return tracks you can load audio effects.

The Device View.

Stacking the Clip View and Device View lets you see both at once. To do so, click the triangle toggles beside the Clip View and Device View Selectors, sitting to the left of the Mixer View toggle down in Live's bottom-right corner. The keyboard shortcuts CtrlAlt3 (Win) / CmdOption3 (Mac) reveal the Clip View and CtrlAlt4 (Win) / CmdOption4 (Mac) reveal the Device View.

Stacked Clip and Device View.

### 3.13 Scale Awareness

With Live's scale options you can pin any clip to a scale you prefer, and you can apply scales across Live's effects and devices too. The moment you switch on Scale Mode for a particular clip, effect, or device, it turns _scale aware_.

Scale awareness on clips can be switched on through the Scale Mode toggle on the Control Bar or straight from the Clip View. The Root Note and Scale Name choosers beside the Scale Mode toggle let you pick a root key and a scale type. Whatever scale settings you choose apply to the selected clip, or — when nothing is selected — to whatever clips you create next.

The Scale Mode Toggle and Scale Choosers in the Clip View.

Whatever scale settings any selected clip carries are reflected by the Scale Mode controls on the Control Bar. Those same controls can flip Scale Mode on/off or assign one shared scale across several selected clips at once.

Scale Options in the Control Bar.

Inside the Clip View, switching on Scale Mode causes the [Fold to Scale and Highlight Scale options](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/editing-midi/#folding-and-scales) to surface in [the MIDI Note Editor](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/editing-midi/#the-midi-note-editor-layout). With Fold to Scale switched on, the editor presents only the key tracks tied to notes within the scale. With Highlight Scale switched on, those scale-note key tracks get tinted purple — the hue that marks scale awareness right across Live.

While a scale is in force, every pitch-related parameter throughout MIDI Tools and the Pitch and Time Utilities panel adopts that chosen scale as well.

A Use Current Scale toggle appears in the device title bars of Live's [Arpeggiator](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-midi-effect-reference/#arpeggiator), [Chord](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-midi-effect-reference/#chord), [Pitch](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-midi-effect-reference/#pitch), [Random](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-midi-effect-reference/#random), and [Scale](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-midi-effect-reference/#scale) MIDI effects. Flip it on and the clip's active scale takes hold, with pitch-based device parameters then tunable in scale degrees instead of semitones.

You can likewise switch on scale awareness for [Auto Shift's](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-audio-effect-reference/#auto-shift) Quantizer and for the oscillators and filters of [Meld](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-instrument-reference/#meld).

### 3.14 The Mixer

Picture an audio clip sounding on an audio track. That clip's audio signal arrives at the chain's leftmost device. After that device alters the signal, it passes the outcome along to the device that follows, and so the chain continues. In theory there's no cap on how many devices a track may hold. Practically, though, your computer's available power sets a ceiling on how many devices run together at once — a matter worth its own [separate discussion](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/computer-audio-resources-and-strategies/#managing-the-cpu-load). Bear in mind that audio devices always connect to one another in stereo, yet the software's inputs and outputs may be set to mono within the Audio Settings.
