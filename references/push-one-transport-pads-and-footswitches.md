# Push 1 Transport Pads And Footswitches

Open when you need guidance on Push 1 Transport Pads And Footswitches in Ableton Live.

Undo: Reverses whatever you did most recently. Keep Shift held as you tap Undo to perform a Redo instead. Keep in mind that the Undo button on Push 1 hooks into Live’s own Undo system, so it rolls back changes in your Live Set regardless of whether you made them through Push 1.

Delete: While you are in Note Mode, pressing this removes the clip you have selected. Within Session Mode, keep Delete held and tap a clip to wipe that one. Hold Delete and pick out a device or track via Push 1’s Selection Control buttons to remove it. Keep Delete held and lay a finger on an encoder to clear the automation that encoder governs. Where no automation exists for a given parameter, holding Delete and brushing the matching encoder instead returns that parameter to its default.

Quantize — A quick tap quantizes whichever notes are selected, or — if nothing is selected — every note in the clip. Keep Quantize held and strike a drum pad to quantize just that pad’s notes. On audio clips, Quantize operates on the transients. Holding Quantize down opens the quantization settings; once you have adjusted them, a single press leaves that view, and a final tap commits your changes.

Double: copies the contents sitting inside the loop and extends the loop’s length to match.

#### 34.15.0.9 Transport Section

Transport Section.

Fixed Length: Switch this on and every fresh clip spans a set count of bars. Leave it off and a new clip keeps recording right up until you hit Record, New or Play/Stop. Hold the button down and the controls under the display let you dial in that fixed recording span. Turning Fixed Length on _while_ recording stops the recording and loops back over the clip’s final few bars, the exact number governed by the Fixed Length value.

Automation: Flips Live’s Automation Record button on or off. With it active, any tweaks you make to parameters get captured into the Session View clips that are running. To bring back automation you have overridden, hold Shift and tap Automation. Holding Delete together with the Automation button strips out every bit of automation in a clip.

Duplicate: Under Scene Workflow, this spawns a fresh scene that gathers up whatever clips are playing at that moment. Under Clip Workflow, it drops a copy of the clip you have selected into the following slot, all while clips on other tracks carry on playing uninterrupted. Keep Duplicate held as you tap a Drum Rack pad to copy that pad and drop it onto another spot in the Drum Rack.

New: Hitting New halts the selected clip and gets Live ready to capture fresh material, giving you room to rehearse ahead of an actual take. On a MIDI track that is armed, keeping Record held as you press New fires off [Capture MIDI](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/recording-new-clips/#capturing-midi).

Record: Tap the Record button and recording starts. A second tap halts the recording yet leaves the clip playing. Tap Record once more to switch on overdub mode, which lets you lay further material into the clip as it continues to play.

Play/Stop: Switches the play button found in Live’s Control Bar on and off. Keep Shift held as you press Play/Stop to send Live’s transport back to 1.1.1 without kicking off playback.

#### 34.15.0.10 Touch Strip

Touch Strip.

With an instrument track active, the touch strip controls pitch bend or the modulation wheel depth during live performance, or sets which range of notes is on hand while you step sequence. With a Drum Rack track active, that same strip chooses the Drum Rack bank.

Whenever an instrument track is active, pitch bend is what the strip does out of the box. You can reassign the strip’s behavior by holding Select and tapping it; each tap flips it back and forth between pitch bend and mod wheel. The display flashes the current mode for a moment every time you swap. Do remember that both pitch bend and the modulation wheel are on offer only while you perform instruments live — they are unavailable from within the melodic step sequencers.

#### 34.15.0.11 Pad Section

Pad Section.

What the Pad Section does is dictated by the Note and Session Mode buttons. With Session Mode engaged, you use the Pad Section to fire off clips in Live’s Session View. With Note Mode engaged, the Pad Section’s role shifts according to whichever track type you have selected at the time:

- MIDI track holding an instrument: the pads sound notes. Tapping Note again and again flips between live playing and melodic step sequencing.
- MIDI track holding a Drum Rack: the Pad Section splits up: the lower-left 16 pads trigger the Drum Rack, the lower-right 16 pads set the clip’s loop length, and the top four rows drive the step sequencer. A further press of Note switches into 64-pad mode so you can play drums right across the full 8x8 pad grid.

#### 34.15.0.12 Scene/Grid Section

Scene/Grid Section.

These buttons, too, take on different jobs according to whether Session Mode or Note Mode is active. Under Session Mode they fire off Session View scenes; keep the Select button held as you press a Scene button to highlight a scene without actually triggering it. Under Note Mode, the Scene/Grid Section fixes the rhythmic resolution of the step sequencer grid, along with the resolution of repeated notes whenever Repeat is switched on.

#### 34.15.0.13 Using Footswitches with Push 1

A pair of ports along the rear of Push 1 let you hook up momentary footswitches. Footswitch 1 serves as a sustain pedal, while Footswitch 2 hands you hands-free command over Push 1’s recording. Tapping that footswitch once flips the Record button, moving you between recording/overdubbing and playing back the clip at hand. Give the footswitch a fast double-tap and it works just like hitting the New button.

Be aware that some footswitches end up acting “backwards” — for instance, notes might hold only while the pedal is _not_ pressed down. You can generally fix this reversed polarity by plugging the footswitch into the port while keeping it held down, though our advice is to opt for footswitches that carry a physical polarity switch.
