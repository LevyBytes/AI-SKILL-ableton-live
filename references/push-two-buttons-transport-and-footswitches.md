# Push 2 Buttons Transport And Footswitches

Open when you need guidance on Push 2 Buttons Transport And Footswitches in Ableton Live.

Solo — Soloes whichever track is active at the moment. Keep Solo held down and tap a different track’s lower display button to put that track into solo. Keep Solo held and strike a Drum Rack pad to isolate that pad instead. If you keep the Solo button depressed for a moment or two, it latches on. After that you can let Solo go and flip any track’s solo state simply by tapping its matching lower display button. Hit Solo once more to release the latch.

Stop Clip — Halts whatever clip is sounding on the active track. Keep Stop Clip held and tap any track’s lower display button to stop the clip running on that track. As long as Stop Clip stays pressed, the lower display buttons flash for every track where a clip is currently sounding. Tap Stop Clip a second time to release it. To silence every clip at once, hold Shift and tap Stop Clip.

Convert — Transforms the active instrument or clip into another form. Exactly what happens hinges on whatever is in focus at the moment you hit Convert:

- If a Simpler is running in [Classic or One-Shot Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#working-with-samples), tapping Convert spawns a fresh MIDI track holding a Drum Rack, the Simpler duplicated onto its first pad. Every other device from the source track is carried over to that new track as well.
- If a Simpler sits in Slicing Mode, tapping Convert will _replace_ that Simpler on its own track with a Drum Rack whose slices are each laid out on separate pads.
- If a Drum Rack is in focus, tapping Convert spawns a fresh MIDI track that holds whichever devices lived on the chosen pad.
- If an audio clip is selected, tapping Convert offers you a choice among:
  - Spawning a fresh MIDI track with a Simpler, or a fresh MIDI track with a Drum Rack whose first pad holds the sample inside a Simpler. The new Simpler retains its Warp Markers and the associated settings, along with all the other devices from the source track.
  - Turning a [Harmony](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/converting-audio-to-midi/#convert-harmony-to-new-midi-track), [Melody](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/converting-audio-to-midi/#convert-melody-to-new-midi-track), or [Drums](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/converting-audio-to-midi/#convert-drums-to-new-midi-track) into a fresh MIDI track.

Double Loop — Repeats the content inside the loop and extends the loop’s span to match.

Quantize — Tap and let go to quantize the chosen notes, or, when nothing is highlighted, every note in the clip. Keep Quantize held and hit a drum pad to quantize the notes on that pad. On audio clips, Quantize acts on transients instead. To reach the quantization settings, press and keep holding Quantize. Once you’ve adjusted them, a single press leaves the settings, and a press-and-release then commits the result.

Duplicate — Under Scene Workflow, Duplicate spawns a fresh scene that gathers every clip currently sounding. Under Clip Workflow, Duplicate drops a copy of the highlighted clip into the following clip slot, leaving any clips already running on other tracks undisturbed. Keep Duplicate held as you tap a Drum Rack pad to copy that pad and place it elsewhere in the Drum Rack. Keep Duplicate held as you tap a track selection button to clone that track. Within Session Mode, hold Duplicate and tap a clip to grab a copy. Keep Duplicate held and tap a different clip slot to drop the clip in.

New — Tapping New halts the chosen clip and readies Live to capture fresh material. That gives you room to rehearse ahead of committing a new take.

Fixed Length — With this turned on, every clip you make spans a set number of bars. With it off, a clip keeps recording right up until you hit the Record, New or Play/Stop buttons. Hold the button down and pick the fixed recording duration with the lower display buttons. Turning Fixed Length on _during_ a recording stops the capture and loops back over the clip’s final bars, the count of which follows the Fixed Length setting.

Automate — Flips Live’s Automation Record button on or off. While it’s active, the parameter moves you make get captured into Session View clips that are playing. To bring back any automation you’ve overridden, hold Shift and tap Automate. To wipe every automation lane in a clip, hold Delete and tap the Automation button.

Record — When Live has the Session View in focus, tap the Record button to start capturing a Session clip. A second tap of Record ends the capture yet keeps the clip playing. A third tap turns on overdub mode, so you can keep recording into the clip as it loops. To arm a track by hand, hold Record and tap that track’s lower display button. When the Arrangement View is in focus instead, tapping Record flips Arrangement Recording on or off. You can likewise kick off Arrangement Recording from the Session View by holding Shift while you tap Record. Keep in mind the roles flip when the Arrangement holds focus; there, holding Shift and tapping Record instead switches Session recording.

Play/Stop — Flips the play button found in Live’s Control Bar. If playback is already halted, holding Shift and tapping Play/Stop sends Live’s transport back to 1.1.1 while leaving it stopped.

Touch Strip — With an instrument track active, the touch strip controls pitch bend or the modulation wheel level during live playing, or it sets the span of available notes while you step sequence. The moment an instrument track becomes active, pitch bend is the default assignment. Switch the strip’s role by holding Select and tapping it; each tap alternates between pitch bend and mod wheel duties. With every switch, the display flashes the active mode briefly. Bear in mind that pitch bend and modulation wheel control work only while playing instruments live, never within the Melodic Sequencer. With a Drum Rack track active, the touch strip instead picks the Drum Rack bank.

Encoders and display buttons — What the encoders and the two rows of eight buttons sitting above and below the display do shifts according to several things, such as the kind of track in focus, the mode you’re in, and so on. No matter the mode, the far-right encoder sets the Main track’s volume, or the Pre-Cue volume while Shift is held. Holding Shift as you turn any encoder lets you make fine adjustments to whatever parameter that encoder happens to govern at the time.

Add Device — Launches Browse Mode so you can drop a fresh device onto the active track. New devices land just to the right of whichever device was previously highlighted, though keep in mind MIDI effects always sit ahead of the instrument in the chain and audio effects always sit behind it.

Add Track — Switches Push 2 into Browse Mode, where you pick the track type to create (MIDI, Audio, or Return) and, if you wish, drop a device onto it right away. Should you tap the Add Track button while a track inside a Group Track is highlighted, the new tracks go straight into that Group Track.

Master — Tap the Master button to jump to the Main track. A second tap of Master takes you back to whatever track you had before.

Scene/Grid buttons — How these buttons behave swaps according to whether you’re in Session Mode or Note Mode. Under Session Mode, tapping them fires Session View scenes. Keep the Select button held as you tap a Scene button to highlight that scene without firing it. Under Note Mode, the Scene/Grid buttons set the rhythmic grid resolution of the step sequencer along with the rhythmic resolution applied to repeated notes whenever Repeat is on.

Setup — Tap to tweak the brightness, how sensitive Push 2’s velocity response feels, and additional [settings](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#setup-menu).

User — Every one of Push 2’s native behaviors can be switched off through User Mode. With that done, Push 2 becomes free to be remapped so it drives other functions in Live or in separate software. The relative encoders on Push 2 perform best under “Relative (2’s Comp.)” mode. To make sure that mode gets picked, rotate the encoder gently leftward as you map it.

Device — Tap to open Device Mode, where the encoders and upper display buttons let you adjust parameters across Live’s [devices and third-party plug-ins](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#working-with-instruments-and-effects). From any other mode, holding the button down flips you into Device Mode for the moment. Let it go and you snap back to whatever mode you came from.

Browse — Tap to open [Browse Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#browsing-and-loading-sounds), the place from which you drop instruments and effects onto your tracks.

Mix — Every tap of the Mix button alternates between Track Mix Mode and Global Mix Mode. Within [Track Mix Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#track-control-and-mixing), the encoders handle volume, pan, and send amounts for whichever track is active. You pick the track using the lower display buttons. From any other mode, holding the button down flips you into Mix Mode for the moment. Let it go and you snap back to whatever mode you came from.

Clip — Tap to open [Clip Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#clip-mode), the place to tweak parameters belonging to the active clip. From any other mode, holding the button down flips you into Clip Mode for the moment. Let it go and you snap back to whatever mode you came from.

Arrow Keys — Move around your Live Set under Session Mode, and step between tracks or between scenes/clips under Note Mode. Within Browse Mode, the arrows shift you across the browser’s columns of items.

Repeat — With Repeat switched on, keeping a pad held re-fires its note over and over. The rhythmic value of that repeated note is set by the Scene/Grid buttons. Hold Repeat down to switch it on just for the moment.

Accent — With Accent switched on, every note that arrives, whether step sequenced or played live, sounds at maximum velocity. Hold Accent down to switch it on just for the moment.

Scale — While Note Mode is active and an instrument track is in focus, tapping this button lets you choose the scale the pads will play. Bear in mind it does nothing when a Drum Rack track is in focus or while you’re in Session Mode.

- Fixed On/Off: With Fixed Mode active, the pad grid keeps each note in place as you switch keys; the bottom-left pad sounds C every time, save for keys lacking a C, where that pad instead sounds whichever note in the key is closest. With Fixed turned off, the grid’s notes slide around so the bottom-left pad always sounds the root of the chosen key.
- In Key/Chromatic: Choosing In Key “folds” the pad grid, so the only notes it offers are those belonging to the key. Under Chromatic Mode, every note appears on the grid. Pads holding notes in the key are lit; pads holding notes outside the key stay dark.
- Scale selection: The upper and lower display buttons let you shift which key the pad grid sounds. Encoders 2 through 7 let you switch among a range of scale types.

Layout — Tap to switch how the pad grid is arranged. Under Session Mode, Layout flips the [Session Overview](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#session-overview) on or off. Under Note Mode, which layouts you get hinge on the kind of track in focus and on the active mode.

- MIDI track containing an instrument — Flips the pad grid between live note playing and melodic step sequencing.
- MIDI track containing a Drum Rack (or a Simpler in Slicing Mode) — Flips between the 64-pad layout and the three-section pad grid (live playing, step sequencing, and loop length).

Note — Tap to open Note Mode. Once it’s on, what the pads do is governed by the kind of track in focus. From within Session Mode, holding Note down flips you into Note Mode for the moment. Let it go and you snap back to Session mode.

- MIDI track containing an instrument — The pads sound notes, or sample slices whenever Simpler runs in Slicing Mode. A tap of Layout flips between live playing and step sequencing.
- MIDI track containing a Drum Rack — Under the Loop Selector layout, the pad grid splits up; the lower-left 16 pads trigger the Drum Rack, the lower-right 16 pads set the clip’s loop length, and the top four rows drive the step sequencer. Tap Layout to move to the 16 Velocities layout. There, the bottom right 16 pads stand for 16 separate velocities of the chosen Drum Rack pad. Another tap of Layout brings up the 64-pad layout, letting you play drums over the whole 8x8 pad grid.

Session — Tap to open Session Mode. Once it’s on, the pad grid switches over to firing clips within Live’s Session View. From within Note Mode, holding Session down flips you into Session Mode for the moment. Let it go and you snap back to Note Mode.

Octave Up/Down — With an instrument track in focus, these buttons move the pads an octave higher or lower. With a Drum Rack in focus, they instead slide the Drum Rack’s pad overview by 16 pads either way. Under Session Mode, they move your reach over the Session View by eight scenes in either direction. They sit dark whenever no further octaves remain.

Page Left/Right — While you’re in the drum or melodic step sequencers, these buttons jump to the preceding or following page of steps. Under Session Mode, they slide your reach over the Session View by eight tracks to the left or right.

Shift — Keep Shift held as you tap other buttons to reach their extra functions. Keep Shift held as you turn encoders to get finer resolution.

Select — Under Session Mode, keep Select held and tap a clip to highlight it while leaving it unfired. Doing so also brings the clip’s name onto the display. Under Note Mode, keep Select held and tap a Drum Rack pad to highlight that pad without sounding it.

#### 35.17.0.1 Using Footswitches with Push 2

Around the rear of Push 2 sit two ports for plugging in momentary footswitches. Footswitch 1 behaves as a sustain pedal. Footswitch 2 hands you hands-free command over Push 2’s recording features. Tapping that footswitch once flips the Record button, moving you between recording/overdubbing and playing back the active clip. A quick double tap of the footswitch matches a press of the New button.

Keep in mind that some footswitches act “backwards”; for instance, notes might sustain only while the pedal stays *un*pressed. You can generally fix a footswitch’s polarity by plugging it into the port while keeping it held down, though our advice is to choose footswitches that carry a physical polarity switch.
