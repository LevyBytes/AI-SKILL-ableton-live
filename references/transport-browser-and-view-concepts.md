# Transport Browser And View Concepts

Open when you need guidance on Transport Browser And View Concepts in Ableton Live.

Context: [Live Concepts](live-concepts.md#3-live-concepts).

<a id="source-31-the-control-bar"></a>
### 3.1 The Control Bar

Live's playback and tempo handles live on the Control Bar, alongside extra widgets that let you tweak scale and MIDI behavior, watch CPU load, and flip back and forth between the Session and Arrangement View.

The Control Bar.

Nine distinct sections make up the Control Bar:

_Browser Options_ - Here sits the Show/Hide Browser toggle, used to reveal or conceal Live's browser, plus the Browser Config Menu, whose choices let you stretch the browser to full height and surface the Tuning and Groove Pool sections.

_Tempo Settings and Metronome_ - This area gives you the means to switch on [Link](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/synchronizing-with-link-tempo-follower-and-midi/#synchronizing-via-link), dial in your Set's [tempo](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/audio-clips-tempo-and-warping/#tempo) along with the time signature, adjust [the metronome](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/recording-new-clips/#metronome-settings), and switch [Tempo Follower](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/synchronizing-with-link-tempo-follower-and-midi/#synchronizing-via-tempo-follower) on or off.

_Scale Settings_ - Whatever [scale settings](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#scale-awareness) the active clip carries are mirrored here. Whatever you adjust in this area carries over to the clip or clip slot you've highlighted, plus any clips you make afterward and any clip slots you go on to choose.

_Follow and Arrangement Position_ - From here you can switch [Follow](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#navigation-and-zooming) on or off, plus inspect and tweak where you currently sit in the Arrangement.

_Transport Controls_ - The widgets that begin or halt playback, and that kick off Arrangement recording, sit in this section.

_Automation and Capture MIDI_ - Found here are the means to adjust MIDI overdub behavior, arm [automation](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/automation-and-editing-envelopes/#automation-and-editing-envelopes), restore automation for parameters you've currently overriden, [capture MIDI](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/recording-new-clips/#capturing-midi), and begin Session recording.

_Arrangement Loop Settings_ - Switching on and setting up [the Arrangement loop](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#the-arrangement-loop), plus the recording punch-in and punch-out points, happens in this section.

_MIDI and CPU Settings_ - This area lets you switch on [Draw Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/editing-midi/#draw-mode), turn on the [Computer MIDI Keyboard](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-keyboard-shortcuts/#keymidi-map-mode-and-the-computer-midi-keyboard), flip [Key and MIDI map modes](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/midi-and-key-remote-control/#midi-and-key-remote-control) on or off, set the project sample rate, and keep an eye on CPU load.

_View Selector_ - A single toggle here flips you between the Session and Arrangement View.

<a id="source-32-the-status-bar"></a>
### 3.2 The Status Bar

Handy notices show up on the Status Bar, such as error notices or news about new releases on offer (provided Automatic Updates is switched on in the [Licenses & Updates Settings](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/first-steps/#licenses--updates)).

The Status Bar.

While you edit in the MIDI Note Editor, the Status Bar reports useful facts about a chosen note's spot, pitch, velocity, and probability. Hover the pointer above an insert marker in the Session or Arrangement View and the Status Bar reports exactly where that marker sits.

<a id="source-33-the-browser"></a>
### 3.3 The Browser

Your collection of musical material is reached through Live's [browser](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-the-browser/#working-with-the-browser): the Core Library of sounds shipped with the program, extra sounds you brought in through _Live Packs_, presets and samples you've stored, devices both built-in and third-party, Sets you keep in [Ableton Cloud](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-the-browser/#using-ableton-cloud), files held on Push, and whatever folders you've appended yourself.

The Browser.

To reveal or conceal the browser, use the dedicated Show/Hide Browser toggle on the Control Bar, or press Ctrl+Alt+5 (Win) / Cmd+Option+5 (Mac). The Browser Config Menu likewise offers choices for surfacing more sections or stretching the browser to full height.

<a id="source-34-sound-similarity"></a>
### 3.4 Sound Similarity

Built into Live is sound similarity recognition, which powers a pair of features: Similarity Search and Similar Sample Swapping. Similarity Search locates sounds that resemble a reference file by matching that reference against entries in the Core Library and the User Library. Samples as long as 60 seconds, instrument presets, and drum presets all work with Similarity Search. You can pair it with Live's Similar Sample Swapping feature to substitute sample files with comparable sounds inside Drum Rack, Simpler, or Drum Sampler.

Whenever a compatible file is highlighted in the browser, a Show Similar Files icon shows up beside it.

The Show Similar Files Icon Next to a Sample.

A click on the icon produces a roster of sounds resembling the reference file. Alternatively, right-click an entry and pick Show Similar Files, or press the Ctrl+Shift+F (Win) / Cmd+Shift+F (Mac) shortcut, to call up that roster. Your reference file appears in the search field, with every matching, similar-sounding entry listed underneath it, sorted from closest match to most distant.

Similarity Search Results.

If you store custom User Labels drawn from a set of Similarity Search results, the sound file the search started from gets placed automatically at the head of the list.

For the similarity features to function, sound content must first be analyzed. That analysis runs quietly behind the scenes each time Live turns up fresh user audio files. While background scanning and analysis run, the Status Bar reports where Live's similarity analysis stands, and a Pause button beside the analysis state lets you halt the procedure whenever you like. Keep in mind that content in the Core Library arrives already analyzed.

<a id="source-35-live-sets"></a>
### 3.5 Live Sets

What you build and edit within Live is a document termed a [Live Set](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/#live-sets). Every Live Set lives inside a Live Project — a folder that gathers the materials that belong together. After you store the Project folder, the File menu's Open command lets you reopen the Set later.

A Live Set in the Browser.

You can also reach the Live Project folder and the related files tied to whichever Live Set is open right now through the Current Project label inside Live's Places.

<a id="source-36-arrangement-and-session"></a>
### 3.6 Arrangement and Session

Live's fundamental musical units go by the name _clips_. Each clip holds some musical content: a melody, a drum groove, a bassline, or even a finished track. With Live you can capture and reshape clips, then assemble bigger musical forms out of them: songs, scores, remixes, DJ sets, or live performances.

Clips can be handled in a pair of views: the _Arrangement_, where clips are arranged along a musical timeline that runs linearly; and the _Session_, a real-time-oriented "launching base" for clips. A dedicated play button sits on every Session clip, letting you fire that clip whenever and in whatever sequence you wish. How each clip acts once fired can be set exactly through a [number of settings](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/launching-clips/#launching-clips).

Clips in the Arrangement View (Left) and in the Session View (Right).

You reach the Arrangement by way of the [Arrangement View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#arrangement-view), and the Session by way of the [Session View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/session-view/#session-view).

Running Live in just one window, you flip between the pair of views with the computer's Tab key or with the matching view controls up in the window's top right corner. Running two windows instead, a press of Tab moves the Session and Arrangement from one window across to the other. Bear in mind that with the 'Use Tab Key to Navigate' option switched on in the Display & Input Settings, Tab will no longer flip between Arrangement and Session View. Even so, the shortcuts Alt+1 (Win) / Option+1 (Mac) take you to Session View and Alt+2 (Win) / Option+2 (Mac) take you to Arrangement View. Their Navigate menu entries likewise let you move between the views at any moment.

The Arrangement and Session View Controls.

Since the pair of views serve separate purposes, each keeps its own separate set of clips. Still, it's worth grasping that flipping between the views while playing or recording alters only the UI — the clips currently sounding keep going untouched.

There are handy ways the Arrangement View and Session View work together. For example, you might jam with Session clips while you [record a log of the improvisation](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/session-view/#recording-sessions-into-the-arrangement) into the Arrangement, then polish it afterward. This is possible because _tracks_ tie Arrangement and Session together.
