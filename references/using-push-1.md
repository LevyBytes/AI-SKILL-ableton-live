# Using Push 1 (part 1)

> Ableton Live reference. Original prose; identifiers preserved verbatim.

### 34. Using Push 1

Ableton Push 1 is a songwriting instrument that hands you tactile command over melody and harmony, beats, sounds, and the shape of a track. Within the studio, Push 1 lets you rapidly build clips that fill Live’s Session View while you stay on the hardware the whole time. During a performance, Push 1 becomes a capable tool for triggering clips.

The controls on Push 1 are grouped into several sections, illustrated in the diagram below.

Overview of Push 1’s Controls.

A great deal of how Push 1 acts is governed by the _mode_ that is active, together with the _type of track_ you have chosen. So you can get comfortable with Push 1, this chapter first guides you through several core workflows and then lays out a reference covering every one of Push 1’s controls.

A series of videos is also on hand to help you begin using Push 1. You can find them at https://www.ableton.com/learn-push/

### 34.1 Setup

Getting the Push 1 hardware ready is largely a hands-off process. Provided that Live is open, Push 1 is recognized the moment you plug it into a USB port on your machine. Once it's connected, you can start using Push 1 right away. There are no drivers to install, and you don't have to configure Push 1 by hand in Live’s Settings.

### 34.2 Browsing and Loading Sounds

Sounds can be browsed and loaded straight from Push 1, with no need to reach for Live’s browser. You handle this within Push 1’s Browse Mode.

Press Push 1’s Browse button:

The Browse Button.

While in Browse Mode, the display presents every sound and effect you have on hand, plus the locations drawn from the [Places section](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-the-browser/#places) of Live’s browser. The screen is organized into columns. The leftmost column displays either the particular kind of device you're browsing or the Places label. Every column further right reveals the following subfolder, where one exists. Press the In and Out buttons to move the display rightward or leftward, which lets you reach deeper subfolder levels or fit more presets onto the screen at once.

In and Out Buttons.

Move upward using the Selection Control button — the first row beneath the display — in any column. Drop down a folder using the State Control button at each level, which is the second row beneath the display. Samples and presets drawn from official Packs or Live’s Core Library audition automatically once you highlight them in the browser. To bring in a device preset, press the green button on the right. To bring in the chosen device's default preset, press the green button on the left.

Loading Devices or Device Presets in Browse Mode.

Scrolling rapidly through folders and their subfolders is done with the encoders. Hold Shift as you press the up or down buttons to jump an entire page at a time.

Once you press a device or preset load button, that button changes to amber. This signals that the highlighted entry has been loaded; should you move to another entry, the button reverts to green. Hitting an amber load button brings in the _next_ entry down the list, which makes it fast to audition a series of presets or devices.

Whatever appears in Browse Mode is tied to whichever device you last had selected. Had you been on an instrument, Browse Mode offers up alternate instruments. Had you been on an effect, effects are what you'll find. Beginning from an empty MIDI track, the screen lists every sound, instrument, drum kit, and effect you own, plus [Max for Live device](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/max-for-live/#max-for-live) and [VST and Audio Units plug-ins](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-instruments-and-effects/#using-plug-ins). A folder appears on Push 1 only when it holds items loadable at that moment. As an illustration, the Samples label (along with any folders of your own in Places holding nothing but samples) stays hidden until you browse from one pad inside a [Drum Rack](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#loading-individual-drums).

### 34.3 Playing and Programming Beats

To build beats on Push 1, begin by confirming that Note Mode is turned on.

The Note Mode Button.

Next, go into Browse Mode and pull in a Drum Rack preset from Live’s library.

On a MIDI track that holds a Drum Rack, the 8x8 pad grid of Push 1 can be set up in several distinct ways, governed by where the Note button stands. Each press of that button steps through three separate modes.

### 34.3.1 Loop Selector

With the Loop Selector layout active, the pads split into three zones, which lets you play, step sequence, and set the length of your clip all at the same time.

The Pad Grid When Working With Drums

Mirroring Live’s Drum Rack, the 16 Drum Rack pads sit in the familiar 4x4 grid, which supports playing in real time. Here is what the Drum Rack pad colors mean:

- Bright yellow — a sound sits on this pad.
- Dull yellow — nothing is on this pad.
- Green — this pad is sounding right now.
- Light Blue — this is the chosen pad.
- Dark blue — this pad has solo active.
- Orange— this pad is silenced.

For Drum Racks holding a greater number of pads, reach for the touch strip on Push 1 or, alternatively, the Octave Up and Octave Down keys, shifting up or down in steps of 16 pads. Keep Shift held as you use the touch strip or Octave keys to advance one row at a time.

Keep the Note button held for temporary entry into the [16 Velocities layout](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#16-velocities-mode). You can also pin the alternate layout by holding Shift as you press the Note button. Pressing the Note button once more frees the 16 Velocities layout.

Octave Up/Down Buttons.

### 34.3.2 16 Velocities Mode

Hit the Layout button to move into the 16 Velocities layout. Within this mode, the 16 pads at the lower right stand for 16 separate velocities tied to the chosen Drum Rack pad. Tap any velocity pad to lay down steps at that velocity.

Keeping the Note button held grants temporary entry to the [loop length controls](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#adjusting-the-loop-length). You may also pin the loop length controls down by holding Shift while you press the Note button. A further press of the Note button releases the loop length pads.

### 34.3.3 64-Pad Mode

Beyond the Loop Selector and 16 Velocities layouts, the whole 8x8 pad grid can serve for real-time drum playing too. That comes in handy with very large drum kits, like the ones made through [slicing](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/converting-audio-to-midi/#slice-to-new-midi-track). Press the Note Mode button a second time to switch over to 64-pad mode. A further press of Note swings you back to the Loop Selector layout, so you can return to step sequencing in a hurry. Pad colors in 64-pad mode match those of the three-section layout.

As you shift back and forth across the three layouts, the 16 pads on hand for step sequencing won't update on their own. It may still take a turn of the touch strip or the Octave keys to bring the exact 16 pads you're after into view.

Holding down the Note button hands you brief access to the [loop length controls](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#adjusting-the-loop-length). To keep the loop length controls fixed, hold Shift and press the Note button. Press the Note button one more time to release the loop length pads.

### 34.3.4 Loading Individual Drums

Browse Mode serves equally well for loading or swapping out single pads inside a Drum Rack you've already loaded. To flip between browsing whole Drum Racks and individual pads, press the Device button so the track's devices appear.

Device Button.

Out of the box, the Drum Rack itself is what's chosen, which the arrow in the display points out. To pick a single pad in its place, tap that pad and then press the selection button sitting beneath the pad’s name.

Selecting an Individual Pad in a Drum Rack.

From here, going back into Browse Mode lets you load or swap the sound belonging to just the chosen pad. While Browse Mode is active, tapping other pads picks them for browsing, so you can swiftly load or swap several sounds across the loaded Drum Rack.

#### 34.3.4.1 Additional Pad Options for Push 1

To duplicate a pad onto another spot within your Drum Rack, hold the Duplicate button and tap the pad you wish to copy. Still keeping Duplicate held, tap the pad where the copy should land. Keep in mind that this overwrites the target pad’s devices (and with them its sound), yet it does _not_ overwrite any notes that have already been recorded on that pad.

### 34.3.5 Step Sequencing Beats

Tapping a pad turns it on for step sequencing as well. If you'd rather choose a pad without sounding it, hold the Select button down as you tap a pad.

Select Button.

To lay notes down with the step sequencer, tap the pads in the step sequencer controls so that notes land in the clip wherever you intend. Playback of the clip starts the instant you tap a step. Each step sequencer pad maps to a 16th note out of the box, though you can alter the step size through the buttons in the Scene/Grid section.

Scene/Grid Buttons.

During playback, the step underway is shown by the travelling green pad inside the step sequencer section. With Record turned on, that travelling pad shows red instead. Tapping a step that already holds a note removes that note. Hold the Mute button down while tapping a step to switch it off without erasing it. Hold the Solo button down while tapping a pad to solo that sound.

Mute and Solo Buttons.

It's also possible to fine-tune the velocity and micro-timing of single notes, which the section on [step sequencing automation](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#step-sequencing-automation) explains.

To wipe every note belonging to a pad, hold Delete down as you tap that pad. Bear in mind this removes only the notes that fall inside the current loop.

Delete Button.

Within the step sequencer section, the pad colors stand for the following:

- Unlit — no note sits on this step.
- Blue — a note sits on this step. Deeper blues point to higher velocities.
- Dull yellow — a note sits on this step, yet that note is muted.
- Light Red — should triplets be picked as the step size, the rightmost two columns of pads go red. When that happens, those pads are inactive; only the first six pads of each row of steps remain usable.
  When Triplets are Selected, the Red Steps are Unavailable.

For a thorough rundown on setting the loop length pads, turn to the section titled [Adjusting the Loop Length](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#adjusting-the-loop-length).

### 34.3.6 Real-time Recording

Drum patterns may equally be captured in real time as you play the Drum Rack pads. To record in real time, work through these steps:

- Should you wish to record alongside a click track, press the Metronome button so Live’s built-in click is active
  Metronome Button.
- Next, press the Record button to start recording
  Record Button.

From this point, whatever Drum Rack pads you play get captured into the clip. A second press of Record halts recording while letting the clip keep playing back. A third press of Record switches on overdub mode, which lets you record into the clip as it plays. Further presses keep flipping between playback and overdub.

A press of New halts playback of the clip you've got selected and readies Live to capture a fresh clip on the selected track. This gives you room to rehearse ahead of recording a new idea. Out of the box, pressing New additionally copies every clip running on _other_ tracks into a new scene and keeps them playing without a break. You can alter this behavior by adjusting the Workflow mode found in Push 1’s [User Preferences](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#setting-user-preferences).

New Button.

### 34.3.7 Fixed Length Recording

Press the Fixed Length button so that new clips take on a length you've decided in advance.

Fixed Length Button.

Hold Fixed Length down to choose the recording length.

Fixed Length Recording Options.

With Fixed Length turned off, a new clip carries on recording right up until you press the Record, New or Play/Stop buttons.

Turning Fixed Length on mid-recording stops the recording and loops the clip's final few bars, the count of which follows the Fixed Length setting.

### 34.4 Additional Recording Options

### 34.4.1 Recording with Repeat

Once Push 1’s Repeat button is turned on, holding a pad down plays or records an unbroken stream of evenly-spaced notes. That proves handy for laying down steady hi-hat patterns, as one example. Adjusting how hard your finger presses the pad shifts the loudness of the repeated notes.

Repeat Button.

You dial in the repeat rate with the Scene/Grid buttons. Keep in mind that Push 1 “remembers” the Repeat button’s on/off state and setting separately for each track.

Tap Repeat and let go quickly and the button stays lit. Press and keep it held, and it switches off the moment you release, which gives you momentary control over repeated notes.

Raise the Swing knob to bring swing into the repeated notes. As soon as your hand touches the knob, the display reports how much swing is set.

Swing Knob.

### 34.4.2 Quantizing

A press of Push 1’s Quantize button locks the notes in the chosen clip to the grid.

Quantize Button.

Hold Quantize down to adjust the quantization options:

Quantization Options.

Swing Amount governs how much swing gets applied to the notes being quantized. Keep in mind the Swing amount may be set either from Encoder 1 or from the dedicated Swing knob.

Quantize To picks the closest note value that notes get quantized toward, whereas Quantize Amount governs how far notes may be shifted away from where they started.

Switch on Record Quantize to have notes quantized automatically as you record, and tune the record quantization value with Encoder 8. Note that these controls line up with the settings of the Record Quantization chooser in Live’s Edit menu, so changes can be made either in Live or on Push 1.

For drum work, hold Quantize down and press a Drum Rack pad to quantize the notes of that drum alone within the current clip.

### 34.5 Playing Melodies and Harmonies

Having put a beat together, your next move is usually a fresh track for a bassline, harmony parts, and so on. Press the Add Track button to drop a new MIDI track into your Live Set.

Add Track Button.

Hold the Add Track button down to choose among Audio, MIDI and Return tracks.

Once a track is added, Push 1 drops into Browse mode, letting you load an instrument straight away. With your instrument loaded, confirm that Note Mode is on.

Be aware that if you press the Add Track button while a track inside a Group Track is selected, the new tracks land within that Group Track.

On a MIDI track that holds an instrument, the 8x8 pad grid of Push 1 sets itself up on its own for playing notes. As shipped, every note across the grid falls in the key of C major. The lower-left pad sounds C1 (though the Octave Up and Down buttons let you shift the octave). Heading upward, each pad climbs a fourth. Heading rightward, each pad is the following note of the C major scale.

Sound a major scale by hitting the first three pads of the opening row, then the first three pads of the row directly above. Keep going until you arrive at the next C:

C Major Scale.

The pad colors keep you oriented while you play:

- Blue — this note is the key's root note (e.g., C).
- White — this note belongs to the scale but isn't the root.
- Green — the note sounding right now (any other pads sounding that same note go green too).
- Red — the note sounding right now while recording.

For triads, give the following shape a try at any spot on the grid:

C Major Chord.

Keeping the Note button held briefly opens the [loop length controls](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#adjusting-the-loop-length), which show up in the topmost row of pads. To fix the loop length controls in place, hold Shift and press the Note button. One more press of the Note button releases the loop length pads.

### 34.5.1 Playing in Other Keys

Press Push 1’s Scales button to switch the chosen key and/or scale.

Scales Button.

With the display together with the Selection and State Control buttons, you can change which key the pad grid sounds. The key in effect is flagged by an arrow within the display:

To start with, the pads and scale selection options point to major scales. You can move to an assortment of other scale types via the first encoder, or via the two buttons sitting below the display at the far left. An arrow likewise flags the scale type in effect.

Key and Scale Selection.

Apart from changing the key, you can also reshape the grid's layout by means of the two buttons over on the far right.

_Fixed Y/N_: While Fixed Mode is engaged, the notes across the pad grid hold their positions when you swap keys; the bottom-left pad sounds C every time, save for keys lacking a C, where the bottom-left pad sounds the closest note in the key instead. When Fixed is off, the notes across the pad grid slide so that the bottom-left pad consistently sounds the root of the chosen key.

_In Key/Chromatic_: With In Key chosen, the pad grid is in effect “folded,” leaving only the key's own notes reachable. Under Chromatic Mode, the pad grid carries every note. Notes belonging to the key are lit, while notes outside the key stay unlit.

Keeping the Shift button held while you're in Scales mode opens up several extra note layout options.

Additional Note Layout Options.

The “4th” and “3rd “options name the note interval on which the grid is founded, whereas the ^ and > symbols name the grid's rotation. As an instance, the standard layout is “4th ^”, meaning each row of pads sits a 4th above the row beneath it. The “4th >” option rests on 4ths as well, but it now travels rightward instead of upward; each column is a 4th above the column at its left. The “Sequent” options arrange all the notes in sequence. Such options come in handy when you require a very wide span of notes, since they carry no duplicated notes.

Your most recent picks among the Scale options — that is, key, scale type, In Key/Chromatic, and Fixed Y/N — get stored alongside the Set. Push 1 reverts to these settings whenever the Set is opened once more.

Every one of the [real-time recording options](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#real-time-recording) offered for drums is offered for melodies and harmonies too, taking in [fixed length recording](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#fixed-length-recording), [recording with repeat](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#recording-with-repeat), and [quantizing](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#quantizing). For fine-grained editing, though, you'll turn to the melodic step sequencer covered in the next section.

The real-time Note Mode offers one editing trick: to swiftly clear every note of the same pitch inside the current loop, hold Delete down and then tap the pad in question.

### 34.6 Step Sequencing Melodies and Harmonies

On top of playing and recording in real time, your melodies and harmonies can be step sequenced as well. Press the Note Mode button a second time to switch over to the Melodic Sequencer. Doing so arranges the 8x8 pad grid like this:

The Pad Grid When Step Sequencing Pitches.

In the Melodic Sequencer, all eight pad rows let you drop notes into the clip. You can set the loop length and reach further step sequencing pages through the [loop length pads](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#adjusting-the-loop-length). The loop length pads can be reached briefly in the top row while the Note button is held.

The loop length pads can also be pinned in place. For that, hold Shift and tap the Note button. Be aware that Push 1 retains this locked/unlocked state per track. Press the Note button again to release the loop length pads.

With In Key chosen, every row maps to one of the pitches on offer in the scale you've currently selected. With Chromatic chosen, notes belonging to the key are lit while notes outside the key stay unlit. The light blue row — the bottom row to begin with — marks the root of the chosen key. Every column of pads stands for a step at the resolution fixed by the Scene/Grid buttons.

Just as in the real-time playing layout, a press of the Octave Up and Down button moves the span of notes on offer. The touch strip can serve to change the range too. Keep the Shift key held while you work the touch strip or press the Octave buttons to fine-tune the pitch range. Once you've adjusted the pitch range, or when you cross between the real-time and step sequencing layouts, the display flashes the available range for a moment.

The Display Shows the Range of Available Notes.

On top of that, brightly-lit touch strip lights point out the note range available at the moment, whereas dimly-lit touch strip lights signal that the clip holds notes inside the matching note range.

A further press of Note switches across to the [Melodic Sequencer + 32 Notes layout](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#melodic-sequencer--32-notes).

Beyond adding and clearing notes, you can also tune the velocity and micro-timing of the notes, which the section on [step sequencing automation](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-1/#step-sequencing-automation) covers.

### 34.6.1 Adjusting the Loop Length

The loop length controls let you fix the length of the clip’s loop and decide which slice of it shows up for viewing and editing in the melodic and drum step sequencers. Every loop length pad answers to a _page_ of steps, and how long a page runs hinges on the step resolution. With drums at the standard 16th note resolution, two pages of steps sit available at once, adding up to two bars. Under the Melodic Sequencer layout, a single page of eight steps is available at once, adding up to two beats. To alter the loop length, hold one pad and then tap a second pad or, to fix the loop length at exactly one page, give the matching pad a quick double-tap.

Each Loop Length Pad Corresponds to One Page.

Keep in mind that the page you _see_ need not be the page you _hear_. As you set the loop length, the pages refresh so the current play position (shown by the travelling green pad in the step sequencer section) stays in view at all times. In certain situations, though, you might prefer to switch off this auto-follow behavior. Say you want to edit one page of a longer loop while still letting the loop play across the length you set. For that, single-tap the pad answering to that page. Doing so “locks” the view onto that page and leaves the loop length untouched. To bring auto-follow back, just reselect the current loop. Note that single-tapping a page lying _outside_ the current loop sets the loop to that page on the spot.

Within the loop length section, the pad colors stand for the following:

- Unlit — this page falls outside the loop.
- White — this page lies inside the loop yet isn't on view at present in the step sequencer section.
- Dull yellow — this page is on view in the step sequencer section but isn't playing at present.
- Green — this is the page playing right now.

Should you need the loop length pads on hand often, you can pin them in place. For that, hold Shift and tap the Note button. Be aware that Push 1 retains this locked/unlocked state per track. Press the Note button again to release the loop length pads.

To copy what's on a sequencer page, hold Duplicate, press the loop length pad of the page you wish to copy, then press the loop length pad of the target page. Bear in mind this leaves any notes already on the target page in place and stacks the copied notes over them. To clear those notes first, hold Delete and tap the loop length pad of that page.
