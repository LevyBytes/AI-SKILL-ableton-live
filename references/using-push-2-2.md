Beyond live playing and recording, melodies and harmonies can also be built up one step at a time. Pressing the Layout button switches over to the Melodic Sequencer, which rearranges the 8x8 pad grid like this:

The Pad Grid When Step Sequencing Pitches.

While the Melodic Sequencer is active, notes can be placed into the clip from any of the eight pad rows. To change the loop length or reach further step sequencing pages, use the [loop length pads](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#adjusting-the-loop-length). Holding the Layout button briefly exposes those loop length pads along the top row.

These pads can also be pinned so they stay put. Hold Shift and tap the Layout button to do so. Push keeps track of whether they are locked or unlocked on a per-track basis. Tapping the Layout button once more releases the loop length pads.

When In Key is active, every row maps to one of the pitches that belong to the scale you've chosen. Under Chromatic, the in-key notes stay lit while out-of-key notes remain dark. By default the bottom row is white, marking the root note of the active key. Resolution is governed by the Scene/Grid buttons, and each pad column stands for one step at that resolution.

Just like the live-playing layout, the Octave Up or Down button moves the band of reachable notes up or down. The touch strip offers another way to shift this range. To move it in octave jumps, hold the Shift key as you slide the touch strip. To nudge by a single scale step, hold the shift key while you press the Octave buttons. A brief readout of the current range appears on the display as you make these changes.

On top of that, the touch strip's brightly-lit segments mark where the playable note range sits right now, and the dimly-lit segments flag ranges where the clip already holds notes.

Tapping Layout once more switches over to the [Melodic Sequencer + 32 Notes layout](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#melodic-sequencer--32-notes).

Notes can be added or cleared, and you can also tweak each note's velocity and micro-timing, which is covered under [step sequencing automation](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#step-sequencing-automation).

### 35.6.1 Adjusting the Loop Length

With the loop length controls you decide how long the clip’s loop runs and which slice of it shows up for editing across the melodic and drum step sequencers. A single loop length pad stands for a _page_ of steps, and how long a page lasts is tied to the step resolution. With drums at the default 16th note resolution, two pages sit on screen together, adding up to two bars. The Melodic Sequencer layout instead presents one page of eight steps at once, which equals two beats. Hold a pad and tap a second one to set the loop length; alternatively, a quick double-tap on a single pad locks the loop length to exactly that one page.

Each Loop Length Pad Corresponds to One Page.

Keep in mind that the page on display isn't always the page being played back. As the loop length is set, the pages shift to keep the live play position (shown by the green pad gliding through the step sequencer section) on screen at all times. There are moments, though, where you'd rather switch off this auto-follow. Say you want to work on just one page of a long loop while letting the whole loop keep playing at its set length. Single-tap the pad for that page to do this. That “locks” your view onto the chosen page without touching the loop length. The Page Left/Right buttons also let you step back to the previous page or ahead to the next.

The Page Left/Right Buttons.

Reselecting the current loop is all it takes to switch auto-follow back on. Bear in mind that single-tapping a page lying _beyond_ the current loop snaps the loop onto that page right away. Holding either of the Page Left/Right buttons is another way to restore auto-follow.

These are the meanings of the pad colors within the loop length section:

- Unlit — the page sits beyond the loop.
- Gray — the page belongs to the loop, yet isn't on screen in the step sequencer section at the moment.
- White — the page is shown in the step sequencer section but isn't playing right now.
- Green — this page is the one currently playing.
- Red — this page is the one currently recording.

When you reach for the loop length pads a lot, pinning them in place helps. Hold Shift and tap the Layout button to do that. Push tracks this locked-or-unlocked setting separately for every track. Press the Layout button a second time to free the loop length pads again. The Page Left/Right buttons also move you back a page or forward a page.

Copying a sequencer page's contents starts by holding Duplicate, pressing the loop length pad for the source page, and then pressing the loop length pad for the page you want it copied to. This leaves any notes already on the destination page intact and layers the copied notes over them. If you'd rather clear those notes first, hold Delete and tap that page's loop length pad.

### 35.7.1 32 Notes

Across the lower half of the pad grid you can play notes live and pick them out for step sequencing. Every pad maps to one of the pitches that the active scale makes available. Hit a pad and it gets selected and sounded. A lighter shade of the track’s color marks the notes you've selected.

Press and hold the Select button as you tap a pad to select it without sounding it.

Here is what the pad colors mean:

- The track’s color — the note is the scale's root.
- Lighter version of the track’s color — the pad is selected.
- Green — the pad is sounding at the moment.
- White — the note belongs to the scale but isn't the root.

The Octave Up or Down button moves the reachable note range up or down. Holding the Shift key as you work the touch strip moves that range in octave jumps. To shift by a single scale step, hold the Shift key while pressing the Octave buttons. The display flashes the current range briefly while you adjust it.

Like the 64 Notes layout, the lower half of the pad grid's notes can be set through the [Scale menu](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#playing-in-other-keys).

### 35.7.2 Sequencer

Tap a step across the top half of the pad grid and every selected note drops onto that step. Steps that hold notes glow in the clip's color.

Hold down a step and you can inspect the notes it holds, shown on the lower half of the pad grid in the lighter version of the track’s color. Tap any of those selected notes to strip it out of the step.

Hold several steps at once and the selected notes land on all of them. With Duplicate held, press a step to grab its notes, then press a different step to drop them somewhere else in the step sequencer.

- White — the step is selected.
- Light gray — the step holds a note, but that note is muted.
- Gray — the pad has nothing on it.
- Unlit — when triplets are chosen as the step size, the rightmost two pad columns go dark. Those pads do nothing in that case; only the first six pads of each step row remain usable.

### 35.8 Working with Samples

There are many ways to trigger samples from the pads on Push 2, with thorough yet approachable command over sample parameters straight from the encoders and display. Simpler is the instrument behind Push 2’s sample playback, and it's worth working through the in-depth [Simpler](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-instrument-reference/#simpler) chapter to get the full picture of what it does.

You can begin with a sample by either creating a fresh MIDI track or pressing Browse to drop into Browse Mode on a MIDI track you already have. An empty Simpler can be loaded onto a track, but nothing plays until a sample is present. Push 2’s display lets you know the Simpler holds no sample and nudges you to go browse for one:

Press Browse to Load a Sample in an Empty Simpler.

Once a sample is loaded and you flip to Device View, the sample’s waveform appears on Push 2’s display next to several parameters for rapidly tuning how playback behaves. This is Simpler’s main control bank.

Simpler’s Main Parameter Bank in the Display.

Out of the box, Simpler picks certain parameter settings on its own according to how long the loaded sample is. Short samples, for instance, fire once per trigger, whereas long ones are set to loop and _warp_. A warped sample plays at your Set's tempo no matter which note you hit. Drag a warped clip into Simpler — out of an audio track, from the browser, or off your desktop — and it retains whatever warp settings and markers the source clip carried. The [Audio Clips, Tempo, and Warping](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/audio-clips-tempo-and-warping/#audio-clips-tempo-and-warping) chapter goes deeper on warping.

The Mode control is the key parameter steering how Simpler handles samples, since it picks which of Simpler’s three playback modes is in effect.

Simpler’s Mode Parameter.

- _Classic Playback Mode_ is Simpler's out-of-the-box choice, tuned toward building “conventional” melodic and harmonic instruments from pitched samples. A full ADSR envelope is on board and looping is supported, letting a sample hold for as long as the note stays down. Classic Mode runs polyphonic by default, and its pad grid follows the same layout used for other pitched instruments.
- _One-Shot Playback Mode_ plays back monophonically only and is geared toward one-shot drum hits or brief sampled phrases. Its envelope controls are pared down and looping isn't supported. By default a triggered note plays the whole sample no matter how briefly the note is held. One-Shot Mode's pad grid sticks with the melodic layout too.
- _Slicing Playback Mode_ cuts the sample into pieces non-destructively so each slice becomes playable from the pads. Slices can be made and shifted by hand, or you can pick from several schemes that have Simpler generate them automatically. This mode suits rhythmic drum breaks especially well.

### 35.8.1 Classic Playback Mode

Simpler’s Main Bank in Classic Mode.

Within Classic Mode, the assorted sample position controls dictate which slice of the sample gets played. Load a drum break with silence at its head, for example, and you can have playback begin just past that silence. The Start control fixes the exact spot in the sample where playback can begin, and the End control fixes where it can stop; together these parameters mark out the _region_ you're able to work within. S Start and S Length are given as percentages of the total sample length that Start and End make available. So if you set S Start to 50% and S Length to 25%, striking a pad plays the third quarter (50-75%) of the region bounded by the Start and End values. S Loop Length sets how much of the available sample (again, bounded by Start and End) loops. Keep in mind this parameter only does anything while Loop is enabled.

Turn the Zoom encoder to magnify a section of the waveform. The display renders the entire sample plus the region that's active at the moment. Rotating the Zoom encoder clockwise increases magnification. Which part of the sample you zoom toward follows the sample position control you touched most recently, such as Start, End, S Start, S Length.

Pressing Simpler’s upper display button drops you into Edit Mode. Inside Edit Mode, the lower display buttons reach further parameter pages while the upper display buttons flip certain settings on or off. A second press of Simpler’s upper display button leaves Edit Mode.

Additional Parameters Available in Edit Mode.

The Loop On/Off button decides whether the sample loops while a pad is held. The Warp as… button tunes the warping of the material between Start and End so it lands exactly inside the chosen number of bars. Live takes its best guess at this figure from the sample's length, and when that guess is off, the ÷2 or ×2 buttons let you halve or double the playback speed.

Crop strips away whatever falls outside the Start and End points, and Reverse runs the whole sample backwards. Both Crop and Reverse work non-destructively; each makes a copy and processes that copy, leaving your original sample untouched.

### 35.8.2 One-Shot Mode

Simpler’s Main Bank, shown in One-Shot Mode alongside the extra Edit Mode controls.

Under One-Shot Mode, the Zoom, Start, and End controls behave exactly as they do in Classic Mode, and so do the Warp as…, ÷2, ×2, Crop, and Reverse buttons.

When Trigger is on, the sample keeps sounding even once the pad is let go; how long you hold the pad makes no difference with Trigger active. The Fade In and Fade Out encoders let you sculpt the sample's volume. Fade In governs how quickly the sample climbs to full volume after a pad strike, and Fade Out starts a fade the set distance ahead of the sample region's end. Hold Shift and press the Play/Stop button to cut off a one-shot sample at once.

When Gate is on, the sample starts fading the instant you let go of the pad. How long it takes to fade away to silence after release is set by the Fade Out time.

The Transpose encoder shifts the sample up or down by as much as 48 semitones (four octaves). Be aware that transposing can alter the sample's timbre quite a lot depending on the Warp Mode in use. The Gain encoder controls the Simpler instrument's overall volume.

#### 35.8.2.1 Legato Playback

Classic and One-Shot Modes offer a distinctive method for repitching a sample in real time while its playback position stays fixed. Essentially it's a playable take on the clip [Legato Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/launching-clips/#legato-mode). Switch it on like this:

1. While in Edit Mode, press the second lower display button to bring up the Global parameter bank.
2. Set the Glide Mode parameter to Glide.
3. Set the Voices parameter to 1. Bear in mind this parameter shows up in Classic Mode only. One-Shot Mode is always monophonic in playback.

From then on, playing the pads legato transposes the sample while the playback position holds steady. You'll get the best outcome with Warp turned on (over in the Warp parameter bank). Complex Pro Warp Mode usually sounds the best for transposing, though it pays to try the various Warp Modes and find the one that suits your particular sample.

### 35.8.3 Slicing Mode

Simpler’s Main Bank in Slicing Mode With Additional Edit Mode Controls.

Within Slicing Mode, the Zoom, Start, and End controls work just as they do in Classic and One-Shot Modes, and so do the Warp as…, ÷2, ×2, and Reverse buttons.

How exactly slices get made is governed by the Slice By chooser:

- _Transient_ - Slices land on the sample’s transients on their own. The Sensitivity encoder governs how keenly Simpler reacts to transient levels in the material, which sets how many slices it generates. Larger numbers mean more slices, topping out at 64 slices.
- _Beat_ - Slices fall on musical beat divisions. The Division encoder picks the beat division Simpler uses to cut the sample region.
- _Region_ - Slices fall on equal time divisions. The Regions encoder picks how many evenly-spaced slices get made.
- _Manual_ - With Manual chosen, nothing is sliced automatically. You build slices yourself by switching on Pad Slicing and tapping empty pads while the sample plays. To make manual slices:

1. Tap a pad holding a slice to start playback from that slice.
2. As the sample arrives at the spot where you want another slice (a drum hit, say), tap any empty pad.
3. A slice gets dropped there and tied to a pad. Whatever pads were assigned past that spot move “upward” across the pad grid.
4. When the loop is sliced to your liking, switch Pad Slicing off.

Across every slicing mode, slices fill in left to right in sets of four beginning at the bottom-left pad. The next four slices sit on the four pads above, and so on. Once the pad grid's left half is full, slicing continues along the bottom row of the right side, once more climbing in groups of four.

Up to 64 Slices Can Be Created.

By default Slicing Mode puts the pad grid into the 64-pad drum layout. The Layout button cycles among 64-pad, Loop Selector, and 16 Velocities modes.

How many slices can fire at once is set by the Playback encoder. Mono is monophonic, so just one pad plays or sequences at any moment. Poly lets several pads fire together. Through keeps things monophonic, but firing a single slice carries playback on through the remainder of the sample region.

The Trigger/Gate switch follows the same idea as in One-Shot Playback Mode, though what reaches your ears hinges on the chosen Playback mode.

Nudge lets you reposition each slice marker. It comes in handy especially for tightening up slices you built in Manual mode. For finer nudging, tap the pad you want to tweak and use the Zoom encoder for a closer view. Hold Shift while working Nudge to make extremely tiny adjustments.

Split Slice drops a fresh slice into the center of the slice you've selected. This too helps in Manual mode, paired with Nudge, for refining slices made through Pad Slicing.

Hold Delete and tap the matching pad to remove a slice, whether it was made by hand or automatically.

### 35.9 Navigating in Note Mode

With a handful of tracks already made, you're free to keep building more. Still, you'll often want to jump between existing tracks to keep developing musical ideas on those instruments and devices. The lower display buttons take you straight to a chosen track, while the left and right arrow buttons step you one track back or forward.

Arrow Buttons.

Bear in mind that picking a MIDI track on Push 2 arms it automatically, so you can play it right away. Over in Live, track Arm buttons turn pink to show they were armed by selection.

Pink Track Arm Button.

Any track can also be armed by hand: hold its lower display button, or hold the Record button and press that track’s lower display button.

This helps when, for instance, you intend to record audio clips with Push 2. Inside Live, a hand-armed track's Arm button shows up red. On Push 2’s display, tracks armed this way carry a distinct icon.

Icon for a Manually Armed Track.

What the Up/Down Arrows actually do is governed by the Workflow mode chosen in Push 2’s [Setup menu](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#setup-menu). Either way, the Up/Down Arrows move a single scene up or down. Under Scene Workflow, the chosen scene fires. Under Clip Workflow, only the selected track’s clip fires, leaving the clips on other tracks alone.

Moving around with the Up/Down Arrows in Note Mode kicks off playback at once, and the fired clip inherits the play position from whichever clip ran on that track before. This matches how things work when clips are set to [Legato Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/launching-clips/#legato-mode) in Live.

### 35.10 Working With Instruments and Effects

A press of the Device button switches Push 2 into Device Mode, where the encoders drive the parameters of Live’s devices and third-party plug-ins.

Device Button.

Within Device Mode, the upper display buttons pick devices on the active track and open their parameters for editing. Whichever device is selected gets highlighted on the display.

Device Mode Settings.

After a device is selected, a further press of its upper display button enters Edit Mode. Inside Edit Mode, the lower display buttons reach the selected device's additional parameter pages.

Device Parameter Pages in Edit Mode.

While in Edit Mode, pressing the leftmost upper display button carries you back up to the top level of Device Mode.

Press the Leftmost Upper Display Button to Exit Edit Mode.

Some devices, like Live’s [Operator instrument](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-instrument-reference/#operator), carry more than eight pages of parameters. With such devices open in Edit Mode, the rightmost lower display button displays an arrow. Press it to scroll onward to the extra pages, then press the leftmost lower display button to return.

Scroll to Additional Pages of Parameters.

### 35.10.1 Adding, Deleting, and Reordering Devices

Press the Add Device button to bring further devices, such as MIDI or audio effects, onto a track. Doing so opens the Browser and lists every device type loadable onto the current track.

Add Device Button.

An instrument can be loaded through the Add Device button as well, but note that it behaves like Browse Mode: the instrument already on the track gets replaced.

Much like Browse Mode, move through the devices in the browser with the encoders or arrow keys, and use the Load button to bring in the chosen device or preset. A device lands to the right of the device that was previously selected, though keep in mind MIDI effects always go ahead of the track's instrument and audio effects always go after it.

Removing a device means holding the Delete button and pressing the upper display button tied to that device.

Deleting a Device.

To switch a device off without removing it, hold the Mute button and press the upper display button tied to that device. A disabled device, with all its parameters, turns gray on the display.

Hold Mute and press that device's upper display button once more to switch a disabled device back on.
