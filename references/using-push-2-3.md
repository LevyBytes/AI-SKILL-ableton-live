### 35.10.2 Working with Racks

[Instrument, Drum, and Effect Racks](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/instrument-drum-and-effect-racks/#instrument-drum-and-effect-racks) make it possible to hold several device chains inside one device. Push 2’s display gives Racks distinctive icons so they stand out from ordinary devices.

An Instrument Rack alongside a MIDI Effect Rack and an Audio Effect Rack.A Drum Rack.

Opening a Rack means choosing it with its upper display button and then pressing that same button once more. The Rack opens up to show the devices belonging to whichever chain is active. On the display, an opened Rack is marked by an underline running all the way to the last of its contained devices. Pressing the upper display button of a selected Rack repeatedly switches it between open and closed. Keep in mind that Push 2 offers no direct way to fold or unfold Drum Racks; whether they read as folded or unfolded on Push 2’s display follows the state you gave them inside Live.

Expand a Rack to Access Its Devices.

While the Rack as a whole is selected, the Rack’s [Macro Controls](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/instrument-drum-and-effect-racks/#using-the-macro-controls) are mapped across the eight encoders. With a Rack opened, choose any device it holds by hitting the matching upper display button. As soon as you switch to another device, the encoders shift to handling that device’s parameters.

The Encoders Adjust Parameters for the Selected Device.

Reaching the devices on extra chains inside a multi-chain Rack is done by holding the Rack’s upper display button. The Rack’s chains then appear along the lower edge of the display, where you pick them using the matching lower display buttons.

Selecting a Chain Within a Rack.

### 35.11 Track Control And Mixing

Hit the Mix button when you want the encoders to handle volumes, pans, or sends.

Mix Button.

Hitting the Mix button repeatedly flips between Track Mix Mode and Global Mix Mode. Within Track Mix Mode, the leading pair of upper display buttons let you switch between the selected track’s mix parameters and that track’s input and output routing options. Choosing the track itself is the job of the lower display buttons. Once Mix is active, the encoders set volume, pan, and send amounts for whichever track is currently selected.

The Mix Controls in Track Mix Mode.

For finer adjustments, keep Shift held as you turn the encoders.

When a Set holds more than six return tracks, the two upper display buttons farthest to the right turn into arrows, letting you page the available parameters either left or right.

Scroll to View Additional Sends.

Once Input & Output is chosen, every routing and monitoring option for the track becomes editable through the encoders.

The Input and Output Settings in Track Mix Mode.

Within Global Mix Mode, the encoders handle volumes, pans, or send amounts across the eight tracks on view. Use the upper display buttons to decide which of these the encoders affect.

Controlling the Pans for Eight Tracks.

When a Set holds more than six return tracks, the upper display button at the far right becomes an arrow, letting you page the available sends rightward. Volumes and Pans, however, stay on screen at all times.

When a Set holds more than eight tracks, the left and right arrow keys let you page the tracks on view either left or right.

Hit the Master button to jump to the Main track. A second press of Master sends you back to whichever track you had selected before.

The Main Track Button.

Be aware that with [Split Stereo Pan Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/mixing/#the-live-mixer) engaged during Global Mix Mode, the display reports the present pan value while the pan dial stays inactive. Under Track Mix Mode, what appears is either the pan control or the stereo pan sliders, which depends on which pan mode is in force.

### 35.11.1 Rack and Group Track Mixing

Push 2’s display marks Group Tracks, along with tracks holding Instrument or Drum Racks, using their own distinctive icons.

A Group Track, plus Tracks holding an Instrument Rack and a Drum Rack.

Tracks of these kinds can be opened up, which lets you reach into them with the Mix Modes to govern the tracks and chains they hold. Open one by selecting it through its lower display button and then pressing that button a second time. The Group Track or Rack then opens to expose the tracks or chains nested inside. On the display, an opened track carries an underline reaching to the last of its nested tracks or chains. Pressing a selected track’s lower display button again flips it between open and closed.

Open a Group Track, or a Track that holds a Rack, to Mix what is inside.

To reach extra chains or tracks that opening up may have pushed beyond the display edge, rely on the left and right arrow keys. With an opened Drum Rack, holding Select and tapping a pad takes you straight to that pad within the mixer, which eases mixing when a Drum Rack is large.

### 35.12 Recording Automation

Adjustments you make to device and mixer parameters can be captured into your clips as automation, letting the sound evolve as the clip runs. Begin capturing automation by hitting Push 2’s Automation button until it lights up red.

Automation Button.

Doing so flips Live’s Session Automation Arm button, so the moves you make on Push 2’s encoders get folded into the clip. Once you’ve finished capturing those parameter moves, hit the Automation button a second time to switch it off. Clearing the moves recorded for a given parameter is done by holding the Delete button while touching the matching encoder. Where no automation exists for that parameter, holding Delete and touching the encoder instead returns the matching parameter to its default value.

A white dot beside a parameter on the display marks it as automated. Where you have overridden a parameter — by tweaking it by hand while not recording — a gray dot appears instead.

Automated and/or Overridden Parameters are Indicated in the Display.

Bring back every automation you have manually overridden by holding Shift and pressing the Automation button.

### 35.13 Step Sequencing Automation

Both the drum and the melodic step sequencers let you set automation on the step you’ve selected.

Exactly which parameters you can reach depends on whichever display mode happens to be active.

Under Clip Mode, holding down one or more steps opens up [note-specific parameters](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#note-specific-parameters).

Under Device Mode or Mix Mode, holding one or more steps lets you build and edit device or mixer automation that applies only to those selected step(s). As you hold a step and turn an encoder, the matching parameter’s automation value gets set just for the slice of time that step stands for. Note too that per-step automation works on any step at all, even a step holding no notes.

### 35.14 Clip Mode

Hit the Clip button to move into Clip Mode, the place where you tweak the various parameters of whichever clip is selected.

Clip Button.

On an audio track with no clip selected, Push will ask you to load a sample.

Loading a Sample into an Empty Clip Slot in an Audio Track.

The colors on the display mirror the clip’s own color, and its name stands out in the top-left corner. A few of the editable parameters vary according to which kind of clip you’ve selected.

Within MIDI tracks that hold Drum Racks, each note shows up in the color of its pad. Across every MIDI track, a note’s velocity is conveyed through how opaque it appears.

Clip Mode With a MIDI Clip Selected.

Whenever a clip plays on the selected track, the display tracks the clip’s song position and scrolls along on its own.

On both MIDI and audio clips, the second upper display button flips Loop on or off. With Loop active, the Loop Position can be set — that is the spot inside the clip where the loop kicks off. Loop Length governs the loop’s span in bars and/or beats, counted from the loop position. Start Offset lets playback begin somewhere other than the loop’s start point, at a chosen spot inside the loop. Should the Loop Position and Start Offset sit together, nudging the Loop Position carries the Start Offset along with it. With Loop switched off, the Start and End position come under your control. That stretch is what plays a single time when the clip launches.

To work in 16th-note subdivisions, hold Shift as you adjust any of these controls.

The first encoder lets you Zoom into or out of the sample. Where it zooms is anchored on whichever position encoder you last touched, for instance Start, Length, Loop.

Clip Mode With an Audio Clip Selected.

On an audio clip you can additionally dial in the clip’s Warp Mode, gain, and transposition. Holding Shift while you move Transpose shifts the value in cents instead of whole semitones. Be aware this likewise moves the Detune parameter over in Live’s Clip View.

### 35.14.1 Using MIDI Tracks in Clip Mode

On MIDI tracks in Clip Mode, Push 2 lets you inspect and fine-tune MIDI you have played or sequenced.

How the display looks shifts according to whichever pad layout is selected at the moment.

Beyond the Zoom, Start, End, and Loop controls offered in [Clip Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#clip-mode), every pad layout adds a further Crop control for removing whatever material sits outside the selected loop.

### 35.14.2 Real-Time Playing Layouts

As you record your live playing through the 64 Notes, 64 Pads or 64 Slices layouts, the Clip Mode display shows the MIDI notes arriving, and it rescales itself so that every note already present fits on screen together. This same “folded” display turns up in [Session Mode](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#controlling-lives-session-view) as well.

Clip Mode’s Folded Display.

### 35.14.3 Sequencing Layouts

Across the melodic and drum step sequencer layouts, a faintly translucent white box on the display marks out the sequenceable area. That box stands for the stretch of pads on which notes can be added, removed, or altered.

Clip Mode’s Sequenceable Area.

Every sequenceable area maps to a single page of steps. Off at the display’s far left, a box signals which pitch range the sequenceable area currently sits in.

When notes get added, lines show up along the display’s far left edge. Those lines flag which pitch ranges hold notes, and you can lean on them to track down and edit notes fast without glancing at your computer screen.

Lines drawn thicker stand for pitch ranges packed with more notes.

Pitch Range and Note Indicators in Clip Mode.

#### 35.14.3.1 Melodic Sequencer

Each page of the Melodic Sequencer layout spans a range of eight notes. You can shift the sequenceable area sideways with the loop length pads or the Page Left/Right buttons, and up or down with the Octave Up/Down buttons or the touch strip.

#### 35.14.3.2 Melodic Sequencer + 32 Notes

Within this layout, the display rescales to bring every note in the clip into view. Selecting a note (or notes) already present in the clip highlights its sequenceable area on the display, which speeds up finding and editing the steps that hold those selected note(s). You move the sequenceable area sideways with the Page Left/Right buttons or the loop length pads, the latter reachable briefly in the fifth row while you hold the Layout button.

Loop Length Pads in the Melodic Sequencer + 32 Notes Layout.

Drums/Slicing Loop Selector and 16 Velocities

Only the chosen pad is displayed by the Loop Selector and 16 Velocities layouts. Move the sequenceable area sideways with the loop length pads or the Page Left/Right buttons, and up or down by picking a different pad.

### 35.14.4 Note-Specific Parameters

In any sequencing layout under Clip Mode, the note settings of each step are open to adjustment. Reach them simply by holding a step down. The display then zooms to whichever page contains that step and hands the controls over to the step’s note settings, with any notes inside the selected step shown highlighted.

Hold a Step to Zoom into its Page and reach Clip Mode’s Note Parameters.

From there, turn the matching encoders to:

- Nudge notes earlier or later in time. The figure shown is the percentage by which the note sits away from the grid line before it, and a negative figure means the note lands ahead of the grid line.
- Set the coarse Length of the notes you’ve selected.
- Fine-tune that length adjustment on the selected notes.
- Alter the Velocity of the selected notes.

These note-specific parameters can equally be set across several steps at once. To do that, hold down every pad you mean to adjust and then turn the encoders; the display then reports the span of values across the selected steps.

You can likewise bring new notes into being with the Nudge, Length, and Velocity values you want by holding an empty step and turning any of these encoders.

With drums, you can set nudge, length, and velocity for every note sounded by one particular pad: hold the Select button, press that pad, and then work the encoders.

### 35.15 Controlling Live’s Session View

Hit Push 2’s Session button to cross over from Note Mode into Session Mode. Holding the Session button down toggles Session Mode for a moment, and letting it go drops you back to Note Mode. By the same token, holding Note while you are in Session Mode toggles Note Mode for a moment.

Session Button.

Under Session Mode, the 8x8 pad grid takes on the job of launching clips while the Scene/Grid Buttons launch scenes. A pad press fires the clip sitting at the matching spot in Live’s Session View. Where the track is already selected, pressing the button captures a fresh clip.

So that you can follow what is happening, the pads glow in assorted colors:

- Whatever color a clip carries, whether it is playing or stopped, shows on its pad.
- Clips that are playing throb in their own color.
- Clips being recorded throb between red and the clip’s color.

Clip colors are editable while you are in Session Mode. For that, hold Shift and press a pad holding a clip, then tap one of the outer-ring pads to assign that color to the selected clip.

Hitting the Mute or Solo buttons mutes or solos, in turn, whichever track is selected. With Mute or Solo held, a press on any track’s lower display button mutes or solos that particular track. Keeping Mute or Solo held down briefly latches it on; you can then let go and toggle tracks on or off merely by pressing the matching lower display buttons. A further press of Mute or Solo releases the lock.

Hitting the Stop Clip button halts the clip playing in whichever track is selected. Hold Stop Clip and press any track’s lower display button to halt the clip playing in that track.

Mute, Solo, and Stop Clip Buttons.

Keeping the Stop Clip button held down briefly latches it on. You can then let go of Stop Clip and halt clips just by pressing the lower display button of the track you wish to stop. With Stop Clip latched, the lower display buttons throb for whatever tracks hold a clip currently playing. A further press of Stop Clip releases the lock.

Halt every clip at once by holding Shift and then pressing Stop Clip.

Push 2 reports back what the software is doing, yet, crucially, the software likewise mirrors what is taking place on the hardware. Inside Live, the clip slots that Push 2’s pad grid is governing at the moment carry a colored border.

Copy a clip by holding the Duplicate button and pressing it. Keep Duplicate held and tap a different clip slot to drop the clip into place there.

Both the arrows and the Shift button widen the reach of the eight-by-eight grid.

- The up or down arrows shift you a scene at a time up or down. The Octave Up or Down buttons jump eight scenes up or down.
- The left and right arrow keys shift you a track at a time left or right. The Page left and right buttons jump eight tracks at a stride.

When you are in Session Mode with the focus on Clip Mode, the display sits “folded,” meaning every note within a clip stays on the display at all times. Seeing all of a clip’s notes lets you recognize it at a glance before you launch it. Select a clip without launching it by holding the Select button while tapping the pad that holds it.

As you record MIDI from an outside source — say a MIDI sequencer or MIDI keyboard — the folded display lets you watch every note coming in.

While in Session Mode, holding the Layout button briefly opens the Session Overview, covered in fuller detail just below.

### 35.15.1 Session Overview

Push 2’s Session Overview is your way to move through sizable Live Sets in a hurry without turning to your computer screen.

Holding the Layout button briefly opens Session Overview, in which the pad grid pulls back to present a bird’s-eye view of your Session View. You may also pin the Session Overview down by holding Shift and pressing the Layout button; press the Layout button once more to release it.

Within the Session Overview, every pad stands for an eight-scene-by-eight-track block of clips, which yields a matrix of 64 scenes by 64 tracks. Press a pad to zero in on that portion of the Session View. As an example, pressing the pad at row three, column one brings the focus onto scenes 17-24 and tracks 1-8. On top of that, each scene launch button stands for a block of 64 scenes (where your Set has them.)

Within the Session Overview, the color scheme works a little differently:

- White: flags the block of clips currently selected, the one the software wraps in the colored border.
- Green: clips are playing somewhere in that block of clips (which need not be the block you have selected).
- No color: that range holds no tracks or scenes.

### 35.16 Setup Menu

Hit the Setup button to set brightness, tune how sensitively Push 2 responds to velocity, and change other settings.

Setup Button.Setup Options.

Raising Pad Sensitivity means less force is needed to reach a higher velocity. At a Pad Sensitivity of 10, any given input velocity yields higher output levels, whereas a setting of 0 yields lower output at that same velocity. Its default (and recommended) value is 5.

Pad Gain lifts or trims the velocity curve as a whole. Larger values push the curve up toward the top of the velocity range, while smaller ones pull it down. The effect of this control is strongest at medium velocities. Its default (and recommended) value is 5.

Pad Dynamics governs how widely velocities spread across the output range. Set to 10, most velocities land as either high or low output with little in between. Set to 0, most velocities land as medium output levels (given Pad Gain stays at 5). Its default (and recommended) value is 5.

Grasping how these three velocity controls relate to one another is easiest if you watch the graphical curve beneath them shift. The horizontal axis plots the striking force (input), and the vertical axis plots the output level.

For a linear velocity curve, put Pad Gain at 4 and Pad Dynamics at 7.

Display Light governs how bright Push 2’s display is, whereas LED Brightness governs the brightness of the pads and buttons. When LED Brightness sits very low, pads can show up in the wrong color. For both controls the default (and recommended) value is 100%.

The Workflow option sets how Push 2 reacts to presses of the Duplicate, New, or Up/Down arrow buttons. The mode you settle on comes down to how you prefer to lay out your musical ideas. Under Scene Workflow (the default), you arrange and move through musical ideas by scene. Under Clip Workflow, your attention stays on just the clip currently selected.

Under Scene Workflow:

- Duplicate spawns a new scene that holds all the clips currently playing and switches over to playing them back without a seam. It matches the [Capture and Insert Scene command](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/session-view/#editing-scenes) found in Live’s Create menu.
- New behaves just like Duplicate, save that it does _not_ copy the clip currently selected. In its place, an empty clip slot is readied so you can start a fresh idea on the current track.
- The up and down arrows step up or down a single scene. The clips in the new scene begin playing back without a seam.

Under Clip Workflow:

- Duplicate places a copy of the currently selected clip into the next clip slot while clips already playing on other tracks keep going. Hold Shift as you press Duplicate to instead spawn a new scene holding all the clips currently playing.
- New readies an empty clip slot on the track currently selected. Clips on other tracks stay untouched.
- The up and down arrows step up or down a single scene. The currently selected track’s clip in the new scene starts playing back without a seam, while clips on other tracks stay untouched.

### 35.17 Push 2 Control Reference

What every button and control does is laid out here.

Tap Tempo — Press it once per beat and the Live Set’s tempo trails your taps. When the “Start Playback with Tap Tempo” button is switched on under Live’s Record, Warp & Launch Settings, your taps can also serve as a count-in: in a 4/4 signature, four taps are needed before song playback starts at the tapped tempo. The encoder sitting above the button moves Live’s tempo in steps of one BPM, and holding Shift as you turn it moves the tempo in steps of .1 BPM.

Metronome — Switches Live’s metronome on or off. Its right encoder dials in how much swing gets applied during Quantizing, Record Quantizing, or whenever Repeat is held.

Delete — Under Note Mode, this button removes the selected clip. With a Drum Rack, hold Delete and press a pad to clear that pad’s notes from the clip (or the pad itself when no notes are recorded). Under Session Mode, hold Delete and then press a clip to remove that clip. Hold Delete and pick a device or track via the upper and lower display buttons to remove the device or track. Hold Delete and touch an encoder to clear the automation that encoder governs. Where no automation has been recorded for a given parameter, holding Delete and touching the matching encoder instead returns that parameter to its default value. Under Simpler’s Slicing Mode, hold Delete and tap a pad to remove that slice. Should the Arrangement View hold focus in Live, a press of Delete removes the clip currently selected in the Arrangement.

Undo — Reverses your last action. Hold Shift while you press Undo to Redo instead. Bear in mind that Push 2’s Undo button calls on Live’s own Undo, so it undoes actions in your Live Set even ones carried out without Push 2.

Mute — Mutes the track currently selected. To mute some other track, hold Mute as you press that track’s lower display button. With Mute held, a press on a Drum Rack pad mutes the pad. Keeping Mute held while you press a step sequencer step switches the step off, and keeping it held while you press a device’s upper display button switches the device off. Keeping the Mute button held down briefly latches it on. You can then let go of Mute and toggle devices or tracks on or off just by pressing the matching upper or lower display buttons. A further press of Mute releases the lock.
