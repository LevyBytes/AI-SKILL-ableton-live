# Using Push 2 (part 1)

> Ableton Live reference. Original prose; identifiers preserved verbatim.

### 35. Using Push 2

As a songwriting instrument, Ableton Push 2 puts tactile control over harmony and melody, drums, samples, sounds, and the overall arrangement directly under your fingers. When recording in the studio, it lets you build clips that fill up Live’s Session View while you stay focused on the hardware itself. For live use, the unit doubles as an expressive tool for performing in the moment, sequencing steps, and firing off clips.

Overview of Push 2’s Controls.

How the unit responds is largely governed by the current _mode_ together with the _type of track_ you have chosen. This chapter introduces several of the core workflows so you can become comfortable with the hardware, after which it lays out a complete reference covering every control on Push 2.

A collection of videos is also on hand to ease your first steps with the unit. You can find them at https://www.ableton.com/learn-push/

### 35.1 Setup

Once you have attached the bundled power supply and run the USB cable to the computer, switch the unit on with the rear power button. Beyond that, configuring the Push 2 hardware happens almost entirely on its own. Provided Live is open, the device gets picked up the moment you plug it into one of the computer’s USB ports. Once connected, you can begin using it right away. There are no drivers to install, and you never have to set the unit up by hand inside Live’s Settings.

Occasionally, firmware revisions for Push 2 ship from Ableton bundled inside Live updates. The very first time you reach for the unit after a fresh Live install, a prompt to refresh the firmware may appear. The hardware guides you through each step of that update.

### 35.2 Browsing and Loading Sounds

Sounds can be located and brought in straight from the hardware, with no need to touch Live’s browser at all. The feature responsible for this is Push 2’s Browse Mode.

Press Push 2’s Browse button:

The Browse Button.

Columns make up the display. The instant Browse Mode opens, the leftmost column presents either the particular kind of device under inspection or the [Collections label](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-the-browser/#collections), giving you fast reach to tagged browser entries. Every subsequent column to the right reveals whatever nested folder follows (where one exists) or else the items inside the active folder. Spin the eight encoders sitting above the display to roll through folders and presets, or step through entries singly with the arrow buttons.

Arrow Buttons.

While you browse, the display grows on its own. Live’s “default” devices can be brought in from the browser hierarchy’s topmost level, and the two upper display buttons farthest right let you jump up or down within that hierarchy in a flash.

Navigate Up and Down in the Browser Hierarchy.

Presets and samples drawn from official Packs or from Live’s Core Library will, by default, sound a preview when you highlight them in the browser. The Preview button switches this previewing on and off.

Preview Button.

To set how loud previews play, keep the Shift button held while you turn the Master volume encoder.

Shift Button.Master Volume Encoder.

Bringing in the highlighted entry is a matter of pressing the Load button.

Loading Items in Browse Mode.

Whatever Browse Mode presents to you hinges on which device sat under selection most recently. Having been on an instrument, you’ll be offered alternate instruments; having been on an effect, the choices will be effects. Begin instead from a blank MIDI track and the display lays out every sound, instrument, drum kit, and effect you own, along with [Max for Live devices](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/max-for-live/#max-for-live) plus your [VST and Audio Unit plug-ins](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-instruments-and-effects/#using-plug-ins).

### 35.3 Playing and Programming Beats

If you intend to build beats with the hardware, confirm first that Note Mode is switched on

The Note Mode Button.

Next, head into Browse Mode, work over to the browser’s Drums area, and pull in any of the Drum Rack presets that ship in Live’s library.

On a MIDI track that holds a Drum Rack, the 8x8 pad grid on the unit can take on several arrangements according to where the Layout button currently stands. Each press of that button rotates among three distinct modes.

The Layout Button

### 35.3.1 Loop Selector

Switch on the Loop Selector layout and the pads split into three zones, so that playing, sequencing steps, and setting your clip’s length can all happen together.

The Pad Grid with the Loop Selector Layout

Mirroring Live’s own Drum Rack, the 16 Drum Rack pads sit in the familiar 4x4 grid that supports playing in real time. Both the display readouts and the Drum Rack pads pick up the track’s color, with small shifts in tone that clue you in to the current state. What the Drum Rack pad colors tell you is this:

- The track’s color — this pad contains a sound.
- Lighter version of the track’s color — this pad is empty.
- Green — this pad is currently-playing.
- White — this pad is selected.
- Dark blue — this pad is soloed.
- Gray— this pad is muted.

Should a Drum Rack hold more pads than usual, the touch strip on the unit, or the Octave Up and Octave Down buttons, will shift you 16 pads at a time. Keep Shift held as you work the touch strip or Octave buttons and the movement drops to one row per step.

Octave Up/Down Buttons.

Keep the Layout button held for temporary entry into the [16 Velocities layout](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#16-velocities-mode). To pin that alternate layout in place instead, hold Shift as you press the Layout button. Pressing Layout once more frees the 16 Velocities layout.

### 35.3.2 16 Velocities Mode

A press of the Layout button moves you into the 16 Velocities layout. Here, the lower-right block of 16 pads stands for 16 separate velocities tied to whichever Drum Rack pad is chosen. Strike any one of those velocity pads to lay steps down at the matching velocity.

The Pad Grid with the 16 Velocities Layout

Keep the Layout button held for temporary entry into the [loop length controls](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#adjusting-the-loop-length). To pin those loop length controls in place instead, hold Shift as you press the Layout button. Pressing Layout once more frees the loop length pads.

### 35.3.3 64-Pad Mode

The full 8x8 pad grid is also available for playing drums in real time. That comes in handy with sprawling drum kits, for instance ones built through [slicing](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/converting-audio-to-midi/#slice-to-new-midi-track). One more press of the Layout button brings you into 64-pad mode.

Shifting between 64-pad mode and either the Loop Selector or 16 Velocities layouts leaves the 16 step-sequencing pads where they were rather than updating them. To bring the exact 16 pads you’re after into view, you might still have to reach for the touch strip or the Octave keys.

Keep the Layout button held for temporary entry into the [loop length controls](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#adjusting-the-loop-length). To pin those loop length controls in place instead, hold Shift as you press the Layout button. Pressing Layout once more frees the loop length pads.

### 35.3.4 Loading Individual Drums

Single pads inside a loaded Drum Rack can likewise be brought in or swapped out through Browse Mode. So that you can flip between browsing whole Drum Racks and lone pads, get into Device Mode first with a press of the Device button. Doing so surfaces the track’s devices.

Device Button.

The Drum Rack starts out selected. Picking a lone pad instead means tapping that pad and then hitting the second upper display button. Alongside the name, a square icon stands for a pad.

Selecting an Individual Pad in a Drum Rack.

Stepping into Browse Mode now lets you bring in or swap only the chosen pad’s sound. That selected pad blinks. With Browse Mode open, tapping further pads picks them for browsing, so swapping or loading several sounds across the loaded Drum Rack goes quickly.

Once the chosen entry has loaded, the Load button is renamed Load Next. Hitting it again pulls in the following list entry, letting you audition samples or presets quickly within your song’s context. The Load Previous button steps you back to the list’s prior entry.

In a live setting especially, picking a pad without sounding it can be desirable. For that, keep the Select button held down while you tap a drum pad or any of the 16 Velocity pads.

Select Button.

Triggering can also be avoided by pressing the lower display button belonging to the Drum Rack’s track. That unfolds the Drum Rack and makes the separate pads selectable through the remaining lower display buttons. The left and right arrow keys carry you to the pad before or after.

Press the Drum Rack’s Lower Display Button to Access Individual Pads.

#### 35.3.4.1 Additional Pad Options for Push 2

Duplicating a pad to another spot in your Drum Rack starts with holding the Duplicate button down and pressing whichever pad you mean to copy.

Duplicate Button.

With Duplicate still held, press whichever pad should receive the copy. Be aware that the target pad’s devices (along with its sound) get overwritten, yet any notes already recorded on that pad are _not_ disturbed.

With one pad selected, its [choke group assignment](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/instrument-drum-and-effect-racks/#drum-racks) becomes adjustable through the first encoder, and the second encoder transposes the pad.

Assign a Pad to a Choke Group or Transpose the Pad.

While you’re on drums, each of the unit’s pads can carry its own color. Recoloring a pad means holding Shift and tapping it, then tapping any pad along the outer ring to set that hue for the chosen pad.

These personalized pad colors get stored and recalled together with your Live Set, though Live itself never shows them. Push 2 is the only place they turn up.

Choose a Color for a Drum Pad.

### 35.3.5 Step Sequencing Beats

A tap on a pad both selects it and readies it for step sequencing.

Laying notes down with the step sequencer means tapping pads in the step sequencer controls to drop notes wherever you want them in the clip. Playback of the clip kicks off the instant you tap a step. Each step sequencer pad maps to a 16th note out of the box, though the Scene/Grid buttons let you reset the step size.

Scene/Grid Buttons.

Reach for the Tempo encoder to dial in the tempo. The tempo shifts by a single BPM with every click of the encoder. Hold Shift as you turn it and the tempo moves in steps of .1 BPM instead.

Adjust the Tempo with the Tempo Encoder.

During clip playback, a green pad gliding through the step sequencer section marks the step now sounding. That traveling pad turns red while Record is on. A note already present under a step gets removed when you tap that step. Keep the Mute button held as you tap a step to switch it off without erasing it. Keep the Solo button held while tapping a pad to solo the matching sound.

Mute and Solo Buttons.

Per-note velocity and micro-timing can likewise be tuned, as covered under [step sequencing automation](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#step-sequencing-automation).

Clearing the whole pattern is done with the Delete button. To wipe every note belonging to one pad, hold Delete and tap that pad. Holding Delete while pressing a pad with no notes in the present pattern strips every device off of that pad.

Delete Button.

Within the step sequencer section, the pad colors signify the following:

- Gray — no note sits under this step.
- The clip’s color — a note sits here; brighter pads stand for stronger velocities.
- Lighter version of the clip’s color — a note sits here, yet that note is muted.
- Unlit — picking triplets for the step size leaves the rightmost pair of pad columns dark. Those pads then do nothing; usable steps stop after the first six pads along each step row.
  When Triplets are Selected, the Unlit Steps are Unavailable.

To read more about working the loop length pads, turn to the section titled [Adjusting the Loop Length](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#adjusting-the-loop-length).

### 35.3.6 Real-time Recording

Playing the Drum Rack pads lets you capture drum patterns live as well. To record in real time, work through these steps:

- Should a click track be wanted, hit the Metronome button to switch on Live’s built-in click. Holding the Shift button while you turn the Master volume encoder sets how loud the metronome plays. Like every Push 2 button that toggles something, the metronome’s button light pulses whenever it is active.
  Metronome Button.
- Next, hit the Record button to start recording.
  Record Button.

Where you’ve switched on a [recording count-in](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/recording-new-clips/#recording-with-count-in) inside Live, a countdown bar travels along the top edge of the unit’s display and blinks in time. That gives you a handy visual cue for the moment to start playing.

Count-In Bar in Push 2’s Display

From here, whatever you play on the Drum Rack pads goes into the clip. A second press of Record halts recording yet keeps the clip playing back. A third press of Record turns on overdub mode, so you can keep recording into the clip as it loops. Further presses keep flipping between playback and overdub. As a clip plays, a slim progress bar shows up in the display marking each running clip’s play position.

Although the pads respond to velocity, you can briefly bypass that sensitivity by hitting the Accent button. With Accent active, every note you play or step-sequence comes out at full velocity (127), no matter how firmly you actually strike the pads.

Enable Accent to Play or Record at Full Velocity.

A quick press and release of Accent latches the button on. A press-and-hold instead switches the button off upon release, which gives you momentary handling of accented notes.

Within 16 Velocities mode, striking any of the 16 velocity pads records the chosen sound at that velocity. Keep in mind that Accent takes priority over this.

A press of New halts playback of the clip currently selected and readies Live to capture a fresh clip on the track currently selected. With this you can rehearse ahead of laying down a new idea. By default, New also copies every clip running on _other_ tracks into a new scene and keeps them playing back without a seam. You can alter this behavior by adjusting the Workflow mode inside the unit’s [Setup menu](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#setup-menu).

New Button.

### 35.3.7 Fixed Length Recording

Hit the Fixed Length button so that fresh clips take on a length you’ve decided in advance.

Fixed Length Button.

Keep Fixed Length held to choose the recording length.

Fixed Length Recording Options.

While Fixed Length is switched off, fresh clips go on recording right up until you hit the Record, New or Play/Stop buttons.

By default, launching a recording with Fixed Length active makes an empty clip of the chosen length and then records from that clip’s start, following Live’s global launch quantization. Turn Phrase Sync on and the unit reads the chosen length as a musical phrase, recording from whichever point in the clip lines up with that spot inside a phrase of that length. As an example, set a fixed length of 4 bars with Phrase Sync on and kick off a recording while Live’s global transport sits at bar 7 and you get an empty four bar clip whose recording begins at that clip’s third bar.

Switching Fixed Length on mid-recording turns recording off and loops the clip’s final few bars, the count depending on the Fixed Length setting.

### 35.4 Additional Recording Options

### 35.4.1 Recording with Repeat

Turn on the unit’s Repeat button and holding a pad down will play or capture an unbroken run of evenly spaced notes. It’s handy, for instance, when laying down constant hi-hat figures. How much pressure your finger puts on the pad governs how loud those repeated notes are.

Repeat Button.

You set the repeat rate through the Scene/Grid buttons. Bear in mind that the unit holds onto each track’s own Repeat button state and setting. A quick press and release of Repeat latches the button on. A press-and-hold instead switches the button off upon release, giving you momentary handling of repeated notes.

Raise the Swing knob to lend swing to those repeated notes. Touching the knob makes the display report the swing amount.

Swing Knob.

### 35.4.2 Quantizing

A press of the unit’s Quantize button pulls notes onto the grid within the chosen clip.

Quantize Button.

Keep Quantize held to alter the quantization options:

Quantization Options.

How much swing gets laid onto the quantized notes is governed by Swing Amount. Bear in mind that you can set the Swing amount through Encoder 1 or via the dedicated Swing knob.

The closest note value notes snap to is set by Quantize To, whereas how far notes may travel from where they started is set by Quantize Amount.

Switch on Record Quantize through the matching upper display button to have notes quantized automatically as you record. Encoder 5 sets the record quantization value. Bear in mind that with Record Quantize on and Swing raised, those auto-quantized notes get _no_ swing applied.

While on drums, hold Quantize down and press a Drum Rack pad to quantize that one drum’s notes alone in the present clip.

### 35.4.3 Arrangement Recording

With Live’s [Arrangement View](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/arrangement-view/#arrangement-view) holding focus in the software, a press of Record flips Arrangement Recording on or off. Throughout the time Arrangement Recording stays on, each move you make on the unit lands in the Arrangement View.

Arrangement Recording can also be fired off while Live’s Session View holds focus by holding Shift and pressing Record. Note that the Arrangement holding focus flips this around; holding Shift and pressing Record then flips Session recording instead.

### 35.5 Playing Melodies and Harmonies

Once a beat is underway, you’ll likely want further parts such as a bassline, harmonies, and so on. Where your Set already holds extra tracks, the lower display buttons or the left and right arrow keys let you move among them.

Switch Between Tracks with the Lower Display Buttons.

Otherwise, a press of the Add Track button creates a fresh track.

Add Track Button.

Adding a track sends the unit into Browse mode, where you choose the track type to add (MIDI, Audio, or Return) and, if you wish, load a device onto that new track in the same step.

Choose a Track Type and Optionally Load A Device.

Note that pressing the Add Track button with a track inside a Group Track selected drops any new tracks into that Group Track.

Once a track exists, recoloring it is possible. For that, hold Shift and press the track’s lower display button. Then tap any pad on the outer ring to give that color to the chosen track.

On a MIDI track that carries an instrument, the unit’s 8x8 pad grid sets itself up for note playing automatically. Out of the box, every grid note sits in the key of C major. The lower-left pad sounds C1 (though the Octave Up and Down buttons let you shift the octave). Each pad going up is a fourth above the last. Each pad to the right is the next C major scale note.

Run through a major scale by hitting the first three pads of the bottom row, followed by the first three pads of the row above. Keep on until the following C arrives:

C Major Scale.

To keep you oriented while playing, the pad colors signal:

- The track’s color — here you have the key’s root note (e.g., C).
- White — a scale note that isn’t the root.
- Green — the currently-playing note (should other pads sound that same note, they go green too).
- Red — the currently-playing note when recording.

For triads, give the shape below a try at any spot on the grid:

C Major Chord.

Keep the Layout button held for temporary entry into the [loop length controls](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#adjusting-the-loop-length). To pin those loop length controls in place instead, hold Shift as you press the Layout button. Pressing Layout once more frees the loop length pads.

### 35.5.1 Playing in Other Keys

Press Push 2’s Scale button to swap the chosen scale and/or key.

Scale Button.

The upper and lower display buttons let you alter the key the pad grid sounds. White marks the key now chosen, while gray marks the remaining key choices:

By default, the scale-selection options and pads point to major scales. A range of further scale types is reachable through encoders 2 through 7. Whichever scale type is chosen gets highlighted.

Key, Scale, and Layout Options.

Beyond swapping the key, the grid’s layout can also be reshaped in several ways:

Working in tandem, the _Layout_ (Encoder 1) and _Direction_ (Encoder 8) controls govern how the pad grid is oriented. Out of the box they sit at a Layout of “4ths” and a Direction of “Vert.” Set that way, each pad runs a 4th above whichever pad lies straight beneath it. Switch the Layout over to “3rds“ and every pad now runs a 3rd above the pad straight below it. With the “Sequent” layout, all the notes line up one after another in order. That layout helps when you require a really wide span of notes, since none of its notes repeat.

Switch the Direction control to “Horiz.” and the pad grid spins 90 degrees. With a Layout of “4ths,” for instance, each pad runs a 4th above the pad on its left.

_Fixed Off/On_: Fixed is toggled on or off by the lower right display button. With Fixed on, changing keys leaves the pad grid notes in their existing spots; the bottom-left pad keeps sounding C, save for keys lacking a C, where the bottom-left pad sounds the closest note in the key instead. With Fixed off, the pad grid notes move so the bottom-left pad always sounds the chosen key’s root.

_In Key/Chromatic_: In Key and Chromatic get toggled by the lower left display button. Choose In Key and the pad grid is in effect “folded,” leaving only the key’s own notes reachable. Under Chromatic Mode, every note lives on the pad grid. Lit pads mark notes belonging to the key, while unlit pads mark notes outside it.

Scale settings ride along with the Set, and the unit comes back to them whenever that Set loads again. Should you have favorite key and scale settings you reach for constantly, store them inside your [Default Set](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/#template-sets). From then on, every freshly made Set arrives with those settings already in place for the unit.

Every one of the [real-time recording options](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#real-time-recording) on offer for drums applies to melodies and harmonies too, including the Accent button, [fixed length recording](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#fixed-length-recording), [recording with repeat](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#recording-with-repeat), and [quantizing](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-push-2/#quantizing). For close-up editing, though, you’ll turn to the Melodic Sequencer that the next section covers.

The real-time Note Mode offers one editing trick: holding Delete down and then tapping a given pad swiftly clears every note of that same pitch inside the present loop.
