# Working With The Browser (part 1)

> Ableton Live reference. Original prose; identifiers preserved verbatim.

### 4. Working with the Browser

The browser is where you reach your collection of musical material: the Core Library of sounds bundled with the application, any extra sounds added through _Live Packs_, presets, samples you have stored, both native and third-party devices, plus any directories on your disk holding samples, tracks, and so on. On top of that, the Places section of the browser lets you reach files held in Ableton Cloud and on Push in Standalone Mode.

Several panels and controls within the browser handle searching, filtering, browsing, and previewing your material. Among them are the _search bar_, _sidebar_, _filters_, _content pane_, and further tools.

The Browser’s Layout.

1.

Within the browser's sidebar you will find the Collections, Library, and Places sections together with the labels belonging to each.

2.

To return to a search or navigation state you visited earlier, use the Browse Back and Browse Forward buttons.

3.

Locating browser items and tagged material is what the search field is for.

4.

Toggling the browser's filters on and off is handled by the Show/Hide Filter toggle, whereas the Filter View menu lets you choose which filter groups show up in the Filter View and switch the Tag Editor, Quick Tags, and Auto Tags on or off.

5.

Within the Filter View sits a collection of filter groups whose attached tags let you hunt for items across your library.

6.

Whenever you search or filter, the Results bar shows up. Its Add Label button lets you store the matches under a custom label, and during filtering the bar reports the count of filters in effect.

7.

Whichever label is active in the browser, the content pane lists its items, or alternatively the matches from searching and filtering.

8.

For samples and clips the Preview tab draws a waveform, and it sounds the audio whenever the Preview toggle is turned on.

To change how the width divides between the browser's left and right panes, drag the central divider line sideways. The browser as a whole can also be enlarged by pulling its right edge rightward or its bottom edge down toward the base of Live's window. Keep in mind that pulling the browser's bottom edge down automatically shuts the Info View, plus whichever of the Clip View or Device View is currently showing.

You can additionally make the browser fill the full height of the program's window through the _Full-Height Browser_ entry in the View menu. With this turned on the browser reaches the base of Live's window, yet the Info View, along with whichever of the Clip View or Device View is active, stay visible.

Using the browser means picking one of the _labels_ out of the _Collections_, _Library_ or _Places_ sections in the sidebar, after which you filter or pick the items shown in the content pane. The search bar is another route for tracking down particular items or tags.

### 4.1 Content Pane

The Content Pane.

The content pane holds a list of the items belonging to a chosen label, or else the outcome of a search or filter. While the Name column stays on screen at all times, you can bring up extra columns by picking them from the _Content Options_ menu.

The Content Options Menu.

After choosing extra columns to appear, you may rearrange them by hand: click a column name and drag it where you want it.

To order the items in the content pane up or down by any column, click that column's name. Be aware that right-clicking a column name likewise brings up the Content Options menu.

Among the entries in the Content Options menu is a toggle for displaying or hiding the file extensions on browser items.

### 4.2 Search Bar

Locate browser material by entering the term or terms you want into the search bar. What comes back are files matching _all_ of your terms rather than _any_ one of them. As an illustration, a search for “electric bass” surfaces only those items whose filenames carry both “electric” and “bass”.

If you want to look through every Library location, choose the All label, or hit CtrlF (Win) / CmdF (Mac), which jumps you to the All label automatically; that same shortcut drops a cursor into the search field too.

The Browser’s Search Field.

Tightening a search further is possible by picking tags out of the various [filter groups](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-the-browser/#filter-groups) so the matches shrink. Say you are after string sounds: enter “strings” in the search field, then click whichever tags you like in the Filters view so that only results carrying those tag descriptions remain.

Searching by Term and Then Trimming the Matches with Tags.

Searching by [tags](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-the-browser/#tags) is another route — type # in the search bar and follow it with a tag name, for instance #Drums. Once you begin typing past the hashtag, auto-complete proposals for tag names show up.

You Can Also Find Tagged Content by Searching For Tag Names.

The x button inside the search bar wipes whatever search terms you entered. Bear in mind this leaves the Filters view untouched. To strip out both chosen tags and typed search terms, reach for the Clear button on the Results bar.

Use the Clear Button to Remove All Selected Tags.

If you would rather search without the mouse, here is a keyboard sequence we recommend:

1. CtrlF (Win) / CmdF (Mac) to set a cursor inside the search field and move browser focus onto the All label;
2. Enter the terms or tag names you are searching for;
3. Down arrow key to move into the search results;
4. Up and down arrow keys to move through the search results;
5. Esc to empty the search field and bring back every item in the All label. Whatever tags were chosen get cleared too.

### 4.2.1 Saving Search Results as Custom Labels

When a given search turns up results you would like to return to easily down the line, build a custom user label holding those results by clicking the Add Label button positioned to the right of the Clear button.

Use the Add Label Button to Save Search Results.

After it is stored, the label and what it holds behave just like any other label in the Library.

A Saved Custom User Label.

Custom labels also let you gather particular items in a single spot for quick reach — much as the Collections labels do — by tagging material with tags of your own and then storing the search results as a fresh label.

Should a custom label you added earlier no longer serve a purpose, removing it is fast through its right-click context menu.

A label's context menu lets you rename labels of both the factory and custom kind. That same context menu also lets you pick a different icon for any label, giving you more room to tailor your browser.

Context Menu Options for a Custom User Label.

### 4.3 Browser History

Moving ahead or back through the browser's history is done with the arrow buttons sitting to the left of the search bar.

Use the Arrow Buttons to Access the Browser History.

The keyboard shortcuts below are available as well:

- Move ahead through the browser's history: Ctrl] (Win) / Cmd] (Mac)
- Move back through the browser's history: Ctrl[ (Win) / Cmd[ (Mac)

Both earlier searches and prior navigation states are part of the history.

### 4.4 Filters and Tags

A collection of filters comes with the browser, letting you search for particular material by way of descriptive tags.

The Browser’s Filter View.

Making up the Filter View section are _filter groups_ together with their attached _tags_. The section can be tucked away with the Show/Hide Filter View toggle, while the Filter View menu beside that toggle lets you decide which filter groups stay on screen.

### 4.4.1 Filter Groups

Every filter group holds a batch of tags, and clicking them trims down what the content pane shows. For choosing more than one tag inside a single group, hold the Ctrl (Win) / Cmd (Mac) modifier as you click.

The Sounds Filter Group and its Associated Tags.

Which filter groups are on offer differs from one label to the next, and the on/off state of filters gets stored and brought back as you move among Library labels. As a result you can tailor exactly which groups each label displays however you need.

Bringing up further filter groups, or all of them along with their tags, is done from the Filter View menu.

You Can Choose Which Filters are Displayed for Each Label.

Right-clicking the name of a single filter group within the Filter View section also lets you conceal that group in a particular label. The _Reset Filter Groups to Default_ entry on the Filter View menu puts the label back to its default selection of shown filter groups.

How many filters are active at the moment is reported by the Results bar. Clicking its Clear button wipes the Filter View, dropping any chosen tags and search terms.

The filtered matches can be kept in a custom user label: just click the Add Label button that sits just past the Clear button.

### 4.4.2 Tags

For rapidly finding or arranging your browser material, tags come in handy, all the more so once you make custom ones of your own. They are equally a fine route to stumbling onto material you have yet to meet — just pick a tag in the Filter View and browse the items tied to it.

Every piece of Live's factory material (that is, material from the Core Library and the Live Packs shipped with each Live edition) carries a batch of descriptive tags. Material from the third-party Packs sold in the Ableton webshop is tagged using the tags held inside the “Sounds” filter group.

The default tags on factory items cannot be stripped off; even so, you are free to attach other tags to those items and to add custom tags of your own with the Tag Editor.

From time to time Live carries out a [sound analysis](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#sound-similarity), and during that routine it tags user samples lasting up to 60 seconds. Whatever tag gets applied is the one the sound analysis judges to fit the sample's sound most closely.

The _Enable Auto Tags_ entry in the Filter View menu switches auto tags on or off, and within the Tag Editor they can be deleted or turned into user tags.

Live additionally tags VST3 plug-ins from their VST3 meta data whenever those plug-ins carry a VST Sub Category lining up with one of Live's categories.

### 4.4.3 Tag Editor

Building tags and attaching them to whatever items live in the browser is what the Tag Editor is for. On top of that, you can set up tag groups of your own to bring further order to your tags library.

Opening the Tag Editor is a matter of picking its dedicated entry from the Filter View menu.

Open the Tag Editor from the Filter View Menu.

After it opens, the tags presently attached to the chosen browser item are shown across the default filter groups.

The Tag Editor.

Clicking the arrow beside a group's name folds or unfolds that group. Whenever a folded group holds an assigned tag, its arrow gets a heavier background to signal this.

A Folded Group Containing Assigned Tags.

Attaching a tag to the chosen browser item, or pulling one off, is done by ticking or unticking the box beside the tag in question. Ticking several boxes lets you put many tags on the one item. If you want to tag a batch of items together, hold Shift, pick a number of files in the browser, and then apply the tags you want.

Custom tags can be slotted into any filter group through the _Add Tag…_ entry sitting at the foot of each group.

Use the Add Tag… Option to Create Custom Tags.

Likewise, groups of your own can be built with the _Add Group…_ entry down at the bottom of the Tag Editor.

Use the Add Group… Option to Create Custom Filter Groups.

After a new filter group exists you are free to add custom tags into it. The moment you attach one of the group's tags to a browser item, that group turns up in the Filter View and in the Filter View menu alike. It is a tidy way to arrange browser items around specific purposes — for example, gathering all your field recordings or vocal samples into their own dedicated groups so they are easy to reach afterward.

Rather than piling on several specialized groups, you may instead attach _subtags_ beneath a default or custom tag. Any tag carrying subtags is termed a _parent tag_. Folding and unfolding a parent tag is done by clicking the arrow next to its name.

Adding a subtag means right-clicking a tag and selecting the _Add Subtag in…_ entry. The freshly made tag then sits nested under the parent tag. Note that the tags in the Creator filter group cannot have subtags created for them.

You Can Add a Subtag to an Existing Tag.

Custom tags and filter groups can be renamed or deleted from their right-click context menus. Keep in mind that none of the default filter groups, nor the tags tied to them, can be edited or removed.

Shut the Tag Editor by hitting the X button in its top-right corner, or by choosing the matching entry on the Filter View menu.

### 4.4.4 Quick Tags

Through the Quick Tags section you can take a fast look at the tags on a chosen browser item, or pin other tags onto it.

The Quick Tags Section.

Whatever tags belong to the chosen item are listed in the Quick Tags section. Adding more tags is a matter of clicking _Add…_ and entering a tag name. The moment you begin typing, auto-complete proposals for tags surface. After you attach a tag, it slots into the Quick Tags section alphabetically next to the factory tags.

Beyond attaching tags that already exist, you can also mint brand-new tags right from the Quick Tags section. For that, click _Add…_ and begin typing a new tag's name. The instant a _Create new tag…_ prompt shows up, hit Enter to add the tag. You are then asked to choose a parent tag or tag group for it.

Select a Parent Tag or Tag Group for a New Tag.

Either pick an existing parent tag or group, or set up a new one. Should you type a name that matches no existing tag or group, the prompt returns and offers you the choice of making a parent tag or a tag group.

Create a Parent Tag or Tag Group.

Stripping a tag you put on an item is done with the X beside the tag's name. Be aware that the default tags on items cannot be taken off.

Tagging or untagging several items works just the way it does with a lone item. With multiple items chosen, the Quick Tags section lists every tag attached to any item in the selection. Whichever tags are not common to all the items get flagged with an asterisk.

Tags Marked with an Asterisk.

Picking a tag name in the Quick Tags section makes the Status Bar report the filter group that the tag belongs to.

By default the Quick Tags section is on view, though you can tuck it away with the dedicated _Open Quick Tags_ entry on the Filter View menu.

The Open Quick Tags Entry in the Filter View Menu.

### 4.5 Collections

Every one of the Collections labels carries a color you can assign, and that color lets you bundle assorted browser items (folders included) together in a single place. With these labels you can swiftly arrange and reach specific browser items — say, the ones you like best or lean on most.

Assigning Collections labels happens through a chosen item's context menu, or by way of the number-key shortcuts 1 up through 7. Press 0 to wipe color assignments.

Be aware that Collections labels may also be applied to several browser items inside a selection. What's more, a color label can go onto items of differing kinds. As an example, one and the same color label can land on a drum sound, a MIDI effect, and a plug-in.

When you click a Collections label in the sidebar, every item carrying that color comes up. Any folders sitting in the Collections labels can be unfolded to expose what they hold.

Within the content pane, square icons mark the color or colors given to each item. Note that even though an item may carry several colors, the content pane will display no more than three of them.

Browser Items and Their Assigned Colors in the Content Pane.

Renaming each Collection label is possible from its context menu, or by hitting CtrlR (Win) / CmdR (Mac).

Decide which labels appear in the browser by clicking the Edit button — it surfaces when you hover the mouse to the right of the Collections header — and then ticking the Show/Hide Label option beside each label.

The Browser's Collections Edit Button.Toggling Collections Between Shown and Hidden in the Browser.

Leave Edit Mode by clicking the Done button.

Note that the moment a hidden, unassigned color gets attached to a browser item, that color's Collections label turns up in the sidebar on its own. That said, visible color labels do not vanish by themselves once all of their assignments are taken away.

### 4.6 Library

A range of labels makes up the Library section, each gathering different browser items grouped by type. The search bar or the filters let you track down and dig through every instrument and sound your library offers. Here is how the Library section breaks down:

- _All_ — every item drawn from each browser label in one combined list, devices leading and file types trailing.
- _Sounds_ — the whole of your [Instrument Racks](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/instrument-drum-and-effect-racks/#instrument-drum-and-effect-racks) and instrument presets, plus any instrument preset carrying a tag out of the Sounds filter group.
- _Drums_ — the whole of your drum presets. That covers default drum devices, drum presets (complete Drum Racks alongside single drum hits), and drum samples (loops as well as one shots). On top of that, anything tagged with the Drums.
- _Instruments_ — Live's default instruments together with their presets, plus the whole of your Instrument Racks. The grouping here is by device rather than by sound type.
- _Audio Effects_ — Live's default audio effect devices and their presets, plus the whole of your Audio Effect Racks, grouped by device.
- _MIDI Effects_ — Live's default MIDI effect devices and their presets, plus the whole of your MIDI Effect Racks, grouped by device.
- _Modulators_ — Live's default modulator devices, plus any device carrying the Modulator tag from the Devices filter group.
- _Max for Live_ — the whole of your [Max for Live](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/max-for-live/#max-for-live) devices and presets, plus any Racks assembled from those devices.
- _Plug-Ins_ — your third-party [VST and/or Audio Units plug-ins](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/working-with-instruments-and-effects/#using-plug-ins).
- _Clips_ — the whole of your Live Clips.
- _Samples_ — the whole of your raw audio samples.
- _Grooves_ — the whole of your [grooves](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-grooves/#using-grooves).
- _Templates_ — the whole of your [template Live Sets](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/#template-sets).
- _Tunings_ — every [tuning system](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-tuning-systems/#using-tuning-systems) in the Core Library plus user-saved .ascl or .scl files.

Out of the box, all of the Library labels show in the browser. Tailor which ones appear by clicking the Edit button that surfaces on hover to the right of the Library header, then ticking or unticking labels.

The Browser's Library Edit Button.Toggling Labels Between Shown and Hidden in the Browser.

Once you are done, click the Done button to leave Edit Mode.

### 4.7 Places

Held within the Places labels are your Live Packs, User Library, Current Project folder, and whatever external folders you have brought into the browser. Beyond that, the matching Library Settings options let you switch on the Splice, Cloud, or Push labels under Places. Doing so opens up the Splice sample library, along with files held in Ableton Cloud and those on your standalone Push in Live.

What the Places section actually holds shifts according to how you have set up your library. Labels you may see include:

- _Packs_ — holds Live's Core Library, any extra Packs you have installed on your own, plus Pack updates and downloads on offer.
- _Splice_ — shows the Splice sample catalog, out of which you can drag samples into Live.
- _Cloud_ — shows synced Sets from Note and Move once Ableton Cloud is switched on in Live.
- _Push_ — lists every file from Push 3 while it is hooked up in Standalone Mode.
- _User Library_ — holds the items you have stored yourself, among them default presets, grooves, your own Rack and device presets, samples, Live Clips, and so on.
- _Current Project_ — lists all the files tied to the [Project](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/#live-projects) presently open.
- _User Folder_ — lists particular folders from any of your disks that you have brought into Live's browser.
- _Add Folder…_ — brings up a window for picking a folder on any of your disks to add into Places.

### 4.7.1 Downloading and Installing Packs in the Browser

Within the browser, the Packs label lists every Pack that ships pre-installed with Live, any Packs you have added on your own, and the Pack updates and downloads on offer.

A Pack you have installed turns up as a folder in the content pane, and unfolding it reveals what that Pack contains. Presets, samples, and Live Clips brought in by Packs likewise surface under the relevant Library labels.

Installed Packs in the Browser.

To see whether updates exist for the Packs you have installed, head to the Packs label and open up the Updates section.

Available Updates for Installed Packs in the Browser.

Packs you own yet have not installed are visible too. Such uninstalled Packs sit in the Available Packs section.

Downloadable Packs in the Browser.

Grab any Packs or updates on offer by clicking the download icon located to the right of the Pack's name.

Press the Download Icon to Download a Pack.

As the Pack downloads, the download icon turns into a pause icon that doubles as a gauge of how far along the Pack's download has gotten.

The Download Progress Is Indicated in Yellow.

If the need arises, you can halt Pack downloads and pick them back up later on. Halt a download by clicking the pause icon. While a download sits paused, the paused icon reverts to a download icon. Pick a paused download back up by clicking the download icon once more.

Be aware that downloading a Pack, pausing, resuming or cancelling a download, or installing a Pack can equally be done by picking the right command from that Pack's context menu.

Context Menu Options for a Pack.

Several Packs can be downloaded together: pick them out in the Available Packs list, then click any one of the download icons. Pausing and resuming the download of several selected Packs is likewise handled through one of their pause icons.

After the download wraps up, install a Pack by clicking the Install button.

Press the Install Button to Install a Pack.

Once the Install button is clicked, Live throws up a pop-up dialog with a progress bar reporting where the process stands.

Knowing a Pack's size ahead of downloading and installing it can come in handy — for instance, if disk space is tight. The browser can be set to report the size of every Pack listed in the Updates and Available Packs sections by picking the matching entry from the Content Options menu.

Show or Hide the Size of Packs in the Browser.

Deleting an installed Pack is done through its context menu, or by selecting it in the browser and hitting the Delete key. Note that any Pack you delete shows up afresh in your Available Packs list.

Out of the box, updates and downloadable Packs appear under the Packs label, but the Updates and Available Packs sections can be concealed. For that, flip the Show Downloadable Packs toggle within the Library Settings.

Show or Hide Downloadable Packs in the Browser.
